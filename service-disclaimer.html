Service transparency & operating boundaries

Clear expectations. Responsible movement.

This notice supplements, and does not replace, the Terms & Conditions, Shipping & Delivery Policy, Refund & Cancellation Policy, Partner Standards, Legal Notice and Privacy Notice. The shipment-specific booking, accepted quote, work order and mandatory law govern an actual transaction.

1. Purpose and scope

This page explains the limits of general website information and digital logistics workflows. It applies to visitors, customers and partners using KRIM's public website or platform information, unless a signed agreement or mandatory law provides otherwise.

It is not a promise that a particular service is available, a substitute for a shipment contract, a legal opinion, an insurance policy or a guarantee of transport performance. Nothing here removes a right or remedy that cannot lawfully be excluded.

2. Service availability, locations and coverage

KRIM is designed as a global-ready logistics operating system with an India-first operating focus. Global-ready technology and market information do not mean that KRIM currently operates, serves or can book shipments in every country, state, city, lane or transport mode.

Availability is shipment-specific and may depend on:

  • A verified provider with the relevant capability, capacity and service area.
  • Origin, destination, route, equipment, handling and delivery requirements.
  • Cargo type, weight, dimensions, value, packaging and risk profile.
  • Permits, licences, registrations, customs processes, insurance and local legal requirements.
  • Provider acceptance, operational conditions, security restrictions and payment/document checks.

A location, service category, partner profile or market page may describe a planned, prospective or conditional capability. Do not treat it as a live service offer unless the platform or authorised KRIM representative confirms availability for the specific shipment.

3. KRIM's role and independent service providers

KRIM provides digital workflows and may support discovery, quotation, communication, coordination, payment-related processes and shipment visibility. The precise role performed by KRIM in any transaction depends on the actual conduct, contractual documents, payment flow and applicable law.

Transport, warehousing, customs-related or other fulfilment work may be performed by independent service providers. Where applicable, the booking documents should identify the contracting party, performing provider, scope of service, custody responsibilities, charges and escalation channel.

Legal classification is fact-specific. Calling KRIM a technology platform, marketplace, coordinator, agent or intermediary does not by itself determine whether it is legally a carrier, common carrier, booking company, broker, e-commerce entity or another regulated participant. Actual activities and applicable law control. Required legal advice, registrations and disclosures must be completed before the relevant regulated activity is offered.

4. Quotes, displayed prices and estimates

A quote request, automated calculation, partner bid, indicative rate or displayed price may be preliminary. It may rely on the shipment details supplied and the information available at the time. It is not binding unless issued or accepted by the authorised contracting party with a clear validity period and scope.

Final charges may depend on verified cargo measurements, route, equipment, fuel or accessorial components, loading/unloading, waiting, storage, tolls, duties, taxes, exchange rates and other disclosed shipment-specific factors. Any change must be explained and handled under the accepted contract and applicable law; charges must not be invented or represented as agreed without a valid basis.

Before proceeding, review the currency, tax treatment, included services, exclusions, validity, payment milestones and party responsible for issuing the invoice.

5. Booking acceptance and service commitment

An enquiry, account creation, quote request, draft shipment, partner opportunity or payment initiation does not alone establish that a shipment has been accepted or that capacity has been reserved.

Before treating a shipment as confirmed, obtain a written booking confirmation or equivalent authorised record stating, as applicable:

  • Customer, contracting party and performing service provider.
  • Origin, destination, route, mode and cargo description.
  • Agreed service scope, pickup/delivery window and key milestones.
  • Price, taxes, payment terms, cancellation terms and additional-charge rules.
  • Required documents, handling conditions, insurance position and claims channel.

If a confirmation is incomplete or inconsistent, seek clarification before tendering cargo or relying on a service commitment.

6. Transit times, ETAs and performance estimates

Transit times, pickup windows, delivery dates, capacity indicators and estimated times of arrival are estimates unless the authorised contracting party expressly confirms a guaranteed service commitment in writing. Estimates may change as new operational information becomes available.

Traffic, weather, accidents, route restrictions, vehicle or equipment availability, terminal and port congestion, inspections, customs holds, labour disruption, security incidents, infrastructure failures, incorrect shipment data and other events may affect performance. KRIM will communicate material updates it receives within its actual role and available information.

7. Cargo eligibility, packaging and declarations

Acceptance is subject to the provider's capability and all applicable laws and safety requirements. Customers must accurately declare cargo description, quantity, weight, dimensions, value, packaging, handling needs and any hazardous, restricted, fragile, temperature-sensitive, perishable or high-value characteristics.

Prohibited or restricted goods may not be accepted without all required legal approvals, specialist handling and prior written acceptance. Examples may include dangerous goods, explosives, weapons, controlled substances, counterfeit goods, cash, live animals, perishables, batteries or other regulated items, depending on the service and jurisdiction. This is not an exhaustive list.

The shipper remains responsible for lawful ownership, accurate declarations, suitable packaging, labels, permits and documents except to the extent a written booking lawfully assigns a task elsewhere. Never conceal, misdescribe or tender undeclared cargo.

8. Tracking, milestones and proof of delivery

Tracking events may be supplied by carriers, warehouses, drivers, terminal systems, integrations or manual operational updates. Event coverage, refresh frequency and location precision vary by provider and service.

Updates may be delayed, incomplete, estimated or corrected. A status such as “in transit”, “arrived” or “delivered” reflects the event recorded in the source system and is not, by itself, continuous GPS proof, proof of cargo condition, proof of legal custody or a guarantee of delivery. Where available, request the underlying consignment record or proof-of-delivery evidence for material disputes.

9. Payment processing, settlement and refunds

Payment status shown by a payment gateway or bank may indicate initiation, authorisation, capture, failure, reversal or refund processing. It does not alone determine whether a logistics service was accepted, performed, cancelled or eligible for a refund. Refund eligibility and timing are governed by the accepted booking, the Refund & Cancellation Policy and applicable law.

Payment providers and banks operate their own systems and timelines. KRIM will not claim that a refund is complete until the relevant transaction status is confirmed. Never share an OTP, password, card PIN or full payment credentials with anyone claiming to provide support.

10. Third-party services, integrations and technology

KRIM may rely on hosting, maps, messaging, authentication, payment, analytics, communications and other third-party systems. These services may have independent terms, service levels, maintenance windows, security controls and outages. A third-party integration does not mean KRIM controls that provider's systems or guarantees their uninterrupted operation.

Platform features may be updated, unavailable, limited or under development. Screenshots, demonstrations, roadmap descriptions and test environments are illustrative unless expressly identified as an active production feature.

11. International and cross-border movements

Cross-border shipments may require customs declarations, classification, export/import licences, permits, duties, taxes, sanctions screening, trade controls, local registrations, insurance and specialist providers. Responsibility for each task must be allocated in the shipment documents.

KRIM's global-ready architecture is not a representation that it has a local office, licence, customs-broker authority, carrier registration, partner, route capacity or operational presence in every jurisdiction. A shipment must not proceed until the required route, provider, documents, permissions and legal checks are confirmed.

12. Customer and partner responsibilities

Customers must provide accurate information, lawful instructions, safe access, suitable packaging, required documents and timely payment, and must inspect cargo at handover. Partners must maintain the capability, authorisations, insurance and safety standards required for the accepted work, provide truthful status updates and promptly report exceptions.

Each party must follow the accepted booking, partner agreement, applicable policies and mandatory law. No party should assume that another participant has accepted a responsibility unless it is clearly recorded in the relevant contract or work order.

13. Loss, damage, claims and statutory rights

Liability for loss, damage, delay, shortage, misdelivery or other service failure depends on the applicable contract, the responsible party's role, evidence, insurance and mandatory law. Notify the designated provider and KRIM promptly, preserve packaging and records, record visible exceptions at delivery and follow the claims process and any applicable notice periods.

This notice does not create an insurance policy, promise compensation, determine a claim or waive any consumer, shipper, consignee or other statutory right. No exclusion or limitation applies to the extent prohibited by law. See the Terms & Conditions and Shipping & Delivery Policy for process details.

14. Information security and privacy

KRIM uses reasonable measures appropriate to its systems and stage of operation, but no internet-connected service can guarantee absolute security, uninterrupted availability or error-free data. Users should protect credentials, use trusted devices and promptly report suspected account misuse.

Personal information is handled under the Privacy Notice. Do not upload unnecessary sensitive information or share passwords, one-time codes, payment PINs or confidential credentials through support channels.

15. Updates, precedence and related policies

KRIM may revise this notice as platform features, providers, service availability or legal requirements change. The date shown at the top is the latest intended revision date. Review the version applicable when you request or accept a service.

If this notice conflicts with a shipment-specific accepted contract or mandatory law, the contract and law prevail to the extent legally permitted. Nothing in this page limits access to a statutory consumer authority, grievance mechanism or court/tribunal with jurisdiction.

Before relying on a service: obtain the written shipment confirmation, verify the named provider and contracting party, check the complete charges and conditions, and ensure that the cargo and route are legally and operationally accepted.
