Cargo care & incident response

When something goes wrong, act quickly.

A clear guide to reporting cargo loss, damage, shortage, delay or delivery disputes, preserving evidence and getting the issue to the right party.

This page explains how to raise a shipment claim or incident report connected with a KRIM workflow. It is an operational guide—not an admission of liability, insurance cover, legal opinion or guarantee of compensation. The accepted booking, transport documents, relevant provider contract, insurance wording and mandatory law determine the rights and responsibilities for each shipment.

1. Scope and guiding principles

This process may be relevant to a customer, shipper, consignee or authorised representative reporting loss, physical damage, shortage, tampering, misdelivery or a material service incident. The person submitting a claim should have authority to act for the party with the relevant contractual or legal interest.

KRIM will review information within its actual contractual and operational role and, where appropriate, help route or coordinate the issue with the identified carrier, warehouse, service provider, insurer or other responsible party. KRIM's involvement in receiving a report does not make it the carrier, insurer or decision-maker for every claim.

No automatic liability decision: Receiving a complaint or evidence does not establish fault, causation, cargo value, coverage or entitlement. Those matters require review under the applicable documents and law.

2. What should be reported?

Physical damage

Crushing, breakage, wetting, contamination, temperature excursion or packaging failure.

Loss or shortage

Missing packages, partial delivery, unexplained quantity discrepancy or suspected theft.

Delivery and custody

Misdelivery, seal discrepancy, unauthorised handover or disputed proof of delivery.

Material delay or incident

Accident, route disruption, prolonged hold or other event requiring urgent cargo instructions.

Report a potential issue even if the full extent of damage or loss is not yet known. Clearly identify what is confirmed and what remains under inspection.

3. Immediate steps to protect people and cargo

  1. Protect life and safety first. For an emergency, injury, fire, spill or immediate danger, contact local emergency services and relevant authorities before contacting KRIM.
  2. Prevent further loss where safe. Move or secure cargo only when safe, lawful and consistent with instructions. Do not put people at risk or disturb an accident scene.
  3. Record the condition. Photograph or video the outer packaging, inner packaging, goods, labels, seals and vehicle/container condition where accessible and lawful.
  4. Record exceptions at handover. Note visible damage, shortage, wetness, broken seals or other discrepancies on the delivery receipt or electronic proof of delivery before signing, where possible. If signing is unavoidable, record the exception in writing and retain a copy.
  5. Preserve the evidence. Keep packaging, damaged goods, seals, labels and relevant documents until the responsible party or insurer confirms whether inspection is required. Do not repair, discard or dispose of items unless necessary for safety or authorised.
  6. Notify promptly. Contact the responsible provider/carrier and KRIM using the channels in the booking documents. Do not wait for the full investigation to finish before giving initial notice.

4. How to submit an incident or claim notice

Use the KRIM Contact page and identify the message as “Shipment claim / cargo incident”. Include the shipment reference and request that the matter be routed to the appropriate operations or claims contact. If the booking documents specify a separate carrier, warehouse or insurer notice channel, notify that party directly as well.

Include the date and time the incident was discovered, the delivery or inspection status, a concise description, known affected items and a safe contact method. Keep a copy of the submitted message and any acknowledgement.

Important: The Contact page is a general enquiry channel, not a dedicated claims portal and not proof that a formal notice has been served on every legally responsible party. Follow the precise notice method and recipient stated in your contract, transport document or insurance policy.

5. Evidence checklist

  • Clear photographs/videos showing damage from multiple angles, including packaging and labels.
  • Package count, item description, SKU/serial or batch number, weight and quantity affected.
  • Outer and inner packaging, pallet condition, seals, container/vehicle number and seal number, if relevant.
  • Delivery receipt, proof of delivery, goods receipt, consignment note or exception notation.
  • Pickup and delivery dates, location, names/roles of persons present and a short chronology.
  • Messages, emails, tracking events, inspection reports, repair estimates or surveyor reports.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and evidence supporting the declared value, with unrelated sensitive information redacted.
  • Mitigation steps taken and reasonable costs incurred to prevent further loss, supported by receipts.

Keep original files where possible. Do not edit photographs in a way that changes the evidence. Record who captured each image and when.

6. Documents that may be required

Depending on the shipment, incident and claim basis, the responsible party may reasonably request:

  • Booking confirmation, accepted quote, work order and applicable service terms.
  • Transport document, consignment note, goods receipt, delivery record and exception report.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase evidence, declared-value record and proof of ownership or authority to claim.
  • Photographs, inspection/survey report, repair/replacement estimate and proof of mitigation costs.
  • For a shortage or non-delivery: package count at dispatch, handover records, seal details and reconciliation evidence.
  • For an insured claim: policy/certificate details, insurer forms and any documents required by the policy.

Only submit documents through a channel reasonably verified as belonging to the relevant party. Redact bank account numbers, personal identifiers and unrelated confidential data unless they are strictly necessary and requested through a secure process.

7. Assessment and investigation

The claim may be reviewed by the contracting party, performing carrier/provider, warehouse, insurer, surveyor or another party with relevant evidence and responsibility. Investigation can include record reconciliation, interviews, physical inspection, route or tracking review, packaging analysis and verification of declared cargo details.

Cooperate with reasonable inspection requests and preserve the goods in their current condition where safe. If an inspection is proposed, confirm who is arranging it, the scope, location, costs (if any) and whether the inspection affects any deadline. Do not assume that an inspection request pauses a statutory or contractual notice period.

KRIM may communicate updates it receives and coordinate information where this is part of its role. It cannot promise that an independent provider or insurer will reach a particular finding or decision.

8. Who is responsible for the claim?

Responsibility depends on the contract, the actual service performed, custody and handover records, applicable transport regime, evidence and mandatory law. The shipment documents should identify the contracting party and relevant service provider. A logistics movement may involve more than one carrier, warehouse, handler or subcontractor.

KRIM's role is determined by its actual conduct and the transaction documents; a general platform description does not decide legal status. If KRIM is the contracting supplier or has a direct legal duty for the relevant service component, that responsibility is not displaced merely because a third party performed part of the work.

Where a provider or insurer is responsible for determining a claim, the claimant may need to submit directly to that party as well as notifying KRIM. KRIM will not represent that it has authority to settle another party's liability unless that authority is expressly established.

9. Insurance and cargo protection

Do not assume cargo is insured because a shipment was booked, a carrier has motor insurance, or an insurance option is mentioned on a website. Cover depends on an actual policy or certificate, the insured party, cargo, declared value, route, period, exclusions, deductibles and compliance with policy conditions.

Check the policy before dispatch: who is insured, what risks are covered, whether loading/unloading and storage are included, territorial limits, claim notification requirements and the insurer's required documents. If cover is arranged by a customer or another party, the claimant should contact that insurer or broker directly and comply with the policy.

KRIM does not provide insurance advice or guarantee that a loss is insured. A claim under a transport contract and a claim under an insurance policy are separate processes and may have different notice periods and decision-makers.

10. Resolution, repair and settlement

Possible outcomes may include an explanation, correction of records, recovery or delivery of cargo, repair, replacement, a contractual credit, a negotiated settlement or a reasoned rejection. The available remedy depends on the responsible party's obligations, evidence, agreed terms, insurance and applicable law.

No settlement, refund, replacement or compensation is approved merely because a claim was submitted or acknowledged. Any offer should identify the party making it, the amount or remedy, conditions, payment timing and whether acceptance affects further rights. Do not sign a release or full-and-final settlement unless you understand its scope and have had an opportunity to obtain independent advice.

Where an amount is offered, verify the recipient and payment method through an independently confirmed channel. KRIM will not ask for an OTP, password, card PIN or remote access to release a claim payment.

11. Notice periods, limitation periods and legal rights

Different transport modes, contracts, jurisdictions and insurance policies may impose different deadlines for reporting visible damage, concealed damage, shortage, non-delivery, delay or commencing formal proceedings. Some deadlines may run from delivery, expected delivery, discovery or another legally defined event.

Do not rely on a single general deadline. This page does not create a universal claims period and does not extend, suspend or waive any statutory, contractual, policy or limitation deadline. Check the exact transport document, policy and applicable law immediately, and obtain independent legal advice where the value or deadline is material.

Nothing in this policy limits a consumer or other claimant's mandatory legal rights, access to a competent consumer authority, court or tribunal, or remedies that cannot lawfully be excluded. Any contractual liability cap, notice requirement or exclusion applies only to the extent legally enforceable in the circumstances.

12. Factors that may affect a claim

Depending on the governing contract and law, an investigation may consider packaging, cargo suitability, accuracy of declarations, inherent characteristics or deterioration, instructions, loading and unloading, custody records, pre-existing condition, delay causation, mitigation, declared value, exclusions and evidence of the loss.

These are issues for case-specific assessment, not automatic grounds to reject a claim. No exclusion is applied where it would conflict with mandatory law or a non-excludable duty. A customer or partner should not assume that a general statement on this page decides the outcome of a particular incident.

13. Personal information and fraud prevention

Claim materials may contain personal, commercial and shipment information. KRIM handles information according to its Privacy Notice and applicable law, and may share relevant information with the contracting provider, insurer, surveyor, payment provider or authorities where necessary and lawful to investigate or administer the claim.

Use only verified support channels. Do not share passwords, OTPs, CVVs, card PINs, bank login credentials or remote-access codes. Be alert to impersonators who demand a fee, tax, gift card or secret code to release compensation. Independently verify any payment or document request before acting.

14. Escalation and contact

If you have not received a meaningful update, reply through the original contact thread or use the Contact page with your complaint or shipment reference, previous contact date and the specific outstanding question. For a consumer grievance, see the Grievance Redressal & Consumer Support page for escalation information.

Where a statutory consumer remedy or external dispute route is available, you may use it independently. An internal review does not prevent you from approaching a competent authority, subject to applicable law and procedure.

15. Quick action checklist

  1. Make people and cargo safe; contact emergency services if necessary.
  2. Notify the carrier/provider and KRIM promptly through the correct channels.
  3. Record visible exceptions on delivery documents before signing where possible.
  4. Photograph and preserve goods, packaging, seals and records.
  5. Collect booking, transport, invoice, delivery and value evidence.
  6. Check the exact contractual, statutory and insurance deadlines immediately.
  7. Keep copies of notices, acknowledgements, inspection records and communications.
  8. Do not accept a final settlement or release without understanding its effect.
