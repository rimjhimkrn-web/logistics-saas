Cargo safety & compliance

Know what can move. Declare what matters.

A clear cargo acceptance policy for prohibited goods, dangerous goods and shipments that require special permissions, documentation, packaging or provider approval.

Safety before dispatchPrior approval required where statedUpdated 4 November 2026

KRIM aims to support safe, lawful and accurately documented logistics. Cargo acceptance is route-, mode-, carrier-, equipment- and jurisdiction-specific. The categories below are practical screening guidance, not a complete legal list. Applicable law, carrier rules, terminal requirements and written shipment-specific acceptance always take precedence.

1. Purpose and application

This policy applies to cargo enquiries, quotations, bookings and movements submitted through KRIM workflows or proposed to a KRIM-connected service provider. It applies to shippers, consignors, consignees, account holders and representatives arranging shipment services.

It covers goods that are unlawful to possess, sell, export, import or transport; dangerous goods; goods requiring licences or permits; and cargo that needs special equipment, handling, temperature control, security or insurance. A shipment may be refused even if an item is lawful in one location when it cannot lawfully or safely move on the proposed route or mode.

2. Understand the three cargo categories

Prohibited

Goods KRIM or the proposed provider will not accept through the relevant service, including unlawful items and items barred by applicable law or carrier rules.

Restricted / conditional

Goods that may be moved only after all required licences, declarations, packaging, permits, safety checks and written provider approvals are confirmed.

Ordinary cargo

Goods that may be considered for a service after normal eligibility, packaging, route, capacity and documentation checks. “Ordinary” does not guarantee acceptance.

Unclear or undeclared

Goods with unknown contents, missing labels or incomplete declarations may be held from acceptance until the shipper clarifies and proves compliance.

3. Prohibited cargo — do not submit

Unless KRIM expressly publishes a lawful, approved service for a category and confirms the specific shipment in writing, the following are not accepted through ordinary KRIM shipment workflows:

  • Illegal drugs, narcotics, psychotropic substances, controlled substances or precursor chemicals where transport is unlawful or unauthorised.
  • Explosives, improvised explosive devices, detonators, ammunition and firearms or weapon components where the service is not specifically authorised and legally compliant.
  • Stolen goods, counterfeit or pirated products, goods infringing intellectual-property rights, and goods obtained or traded unlawfully.
  • Human remains, human organs, blood, infectious biological material or medical specimens unless a specifically authorised specialist service and all legal approvals are confirmed.
  • Radioactive material, highly toxic substances, prohibited chemical/biological agents and other materials that cannot be lawfully or safely carried by the proposed provider.
  • Goods prohibited by sanctions, export controls, import restrictions, court orders or applicable government directions.
  • Live animals, protected wildlife, endangered species, animal products or plants where possession or movement is prohibited or required permissions are absent.
  • Any item that is concealed, falsely described, unlawfully sourced, unsafe to handle or prohibited by the actual carrier, airline, shipping line, rail operator, warehouse or other facility.
Important: This is not an exhaustive inventory and does not mean every item in a broad category is universally prohibited in every jurisdiction. The relevant law and approved specialist provider determine whether a lawful exception exists. KRIM does not offer such an exception unless it confirms one in writing for the shipment.

4. Restricted goods — written pre-approval required

The following categories must be declared before quotation and must not be tendered until the relevant carrier/provider and KRIM (where involved in acceptance) have confirmed the exact service in writing:

  • Firearms, ammunition, weapon parts, sporting weapons, replicas and controlled security equipment.
  • Alcohol, tobacco, nicotine products, vaping products and other excise- or age-controlled goods.
  • Medicines, vaccines, pharmaceuticals, medical devices, clinical samples and temperature-sensitive healthcare products.
  • High-value goods, jewellery, precious metals, cash, negotiable instruments, antiques, art and collectables.
  • Dangerous goods, chemicals, paints, solvents, aerosols, compressed gases, flammable liquids/solids, corrosives, oxidisers and toxic substances.
  • Lithium batteries, battery-powered equipment, power banks, damaged/defective batteries and electric vehicles.
  • Perishable food, fresh produce, frozen goods, meat, seafood, dairy and products requiring hygiene or temperature controls.
  • Plants, seeds, soil, fertilisers, pesticides, animal feed and products subject to biosecurity or agricultural controls.
  • Oversized, overweight, abnormal-load, fragile, breakable, pressurised or unusually shaped cargo.
  • Waste, scrap, recyclable material, used equipment, contaminated goods and products requiring environmental permits.
  • Goods moving under customs bond, temporary admission, duty suspension, export licence or other special customs procedure.

Pre-approval may be refused, limited to a specific route, mode, date, quantity, package, vehicle or provider, or made subject to additional charges and controls. An earlier approval does not automatically apply to a later shipment.

5. Dangerous goods and hazardous materials

Dangerous goods can create fire, explosion, poisoning, corrosion, radiation, environmental or other risks during handling and transport. Classification, packaging, marking, labelling, documentation, segregation, quantity limits and trained-person requirements vary by mode and applicable rules.

Before requesting a quote, the shipper must disclose the proper technical description, classification, UN number where applicable, hazard class/division, packing group, quantity, packaging type, emergency information and any required declaration. Safety Data Sheets (SDS) may be requested, but an SDS alone is not a transport declaration or permission to ship.

Air, sea, road and rail movements can be governed by different instruments and carrier-specific rules. Examples include the applicable ICAO/IATA dangerous-goods requirements for air, IMDG Code requirements for sea, and applicable Indian and destination-country road/rail rules. The current edition and exact legal applicability must be checked by a qualified shipper/provider for each movement.

Never conceal or misdescribe dangerous goods. Do not place batteries, aerosols, chemicals, fuel, gas cylinders or other hazardous items in a package as ordinary cargo without proper classification and written acceptance.

6. Special-handling cargo

Some goods may be lawful but need a service that KRIM may not currently have available. Examples include cold-chain goods, fragile equipment, high-value cargo, oversized machinery, food, live plants, medical products, temperature-controlled goods and cargo requiring security escorts or special lifting equipment.

The shipper must disclose handling temperatures, acceptable excursion limits, shelf life, stacking limits, orientation, humidity, shock/vibration sensitivity, lifting points, security requirements and any other critical handling instruction before a quote is accepted. Do not assume that a provider has specialist equipment or monitoring unless it is confirmed in the shipment work order.

7. Shipper declarations and duty to disclose

The shipper is responsible for providing a complete, accurate and lawful description of every item, including contents hidden inside equipment, kits, packages or consolidated loads. A generic description such as “goods”, “parts”, “samples”, “personal effects” or “machine” may be insufficient where the actual contents require classification.

  • Declare the true contents, quantity, weight, dimensions, value, origin and destination.
  • Identify dangerous, restricted, controlled, high-value, perishable or special-handling items before quotation.
  • Disclose batteries, liquids, powders, magnets, chemicals, pressurised containers and embedded hazardous components.
  • Provide accurate sender, recipient, end-user and end-use details where trade controls require them.
  • Update the declaration if cargo contents, packaging, quantity, route or destination changes.

Do not rely on a carrier or driver to identify undisclosed goods by inspection. A booking is based on the information supplied and remains subject to verification and acceptance.

8. Packaging, marking and labelling

Goods must be packed to withstand normal handling and the reasonably foreseeable conditions of the accepted mode and route. Packaging must contain the goods, prevent leakage or contamination, protect handlers and other cargo, and comply with any special statutory or carrier requirement.

Dangerous-goods packaging, marks and labels must be selected and applied by competent persons under the applicable rules. Do not reuse packaging with misleading hazard labels or remove required warnings. Ensure labels and handling marks are visible, accurate and consistent with the declaration and transport documents.

KRIM or a provider may request photographs, packaging specifications, inspection or repacking before acceptance. Repacking does not cure an unlawful or prohibited shipment.

9. Permits, certificates and supporting documents

Depending on the goods, route and mode, required documents may include licences, permits, product registrations, safety data, dangerous-goods declarations, test summaries, certificates of origin, phytosanitary or veterinary certificates, health certificates, customs declarations, export/import authorisations, end-user statements or waste movement documents.

The shipper must obtain valid documents from the competent authority and ensure they cover the actual goods, quantity, parties, route and date. KRIM does not issue government licences, certify product safety or guarantee customs clearance merely by receiving uploaded documents. A provider may independently verify documents and decline cargo if they are incomplete or inconsistent.

10. Screening, acceptance, refusal and inspection

Every quote and booking remains subject to service availability, route eligibility, provider capacity, legal compliance, cargo screening and accurate documentation. KRIM or a provider may request clarification, inspect cargo where lawful and contractually permitted, place a shipment on hold, refuse collection, suspend transit or decline onward movement where safety or compliance concerns arise.

Acceptance is shipment-specific and must be evidenced in the final booking/work order or other written confirmation from the authorised party. A quote, automated status, payment confirmation, driver arrival or previous shipment does not prove cargo acceptance.

If goods are suspected to be unlawful, undeclared or dangerous, the relevant party may notify authorities or take other action required or permitted by law. Any return, storage, disposal, quarantine or other handling will be subject to legal directions and the applicable contract; KRIM does not promise that refused cargo can be returned to the shipper.

11. Incident, leakage, damage or emergency

If a shipment presents an immediate threat to people, property or the environment, prioritise emergency response and contact the appropriate local emergency services or authorities. Do not touch, open, move or attempt to neutralise an unknown hazardous substance unless trained and authorised.

Notify the carrier/provider and KRIM as soon as safely possible. Provide the shipment reference, location, nature of the hazard and emergency response already taken. Preserve relevant records and follow lawful instructions from emergency responders and competent authorities.

12. International and cross-border movements

Import, export, transit and re-export controls may apply in the origin, destination and transit countries. A good that is lawful to sell or possess in one country may be prohibited, licensed, taxed, quarantined or subject to end-use restrictions elsewhere.

The shipper/importer/exporter must verify applicable customs, sanctions, export-control, product-safety, biosecurity and other requirements and provide accurate declarations. KRIM's global-ready platform architecture does not mean every country, route, product category or customs service is currently operational or approved.

13. Responsibility, costs and consequences

The shipper remains responsible for truthful declarations, lawful ownership and shipment, suitable packaging, required licences and compliance with instructions. The contracting carrier/provider remains responsible for the duties imposed on it by its contract and applicable law. Each party's legal responsibility depends on the actual transaction and cannot be changed by this summary.

Where allowed by contract and law, costs arising from inaccurate declarations, prohibited cargo, failed collection, inspection, storage, return, disposal, fines or other corrective action may be charged to the responsible party. No charge is imposed by this policy alone; the basis must come from the accepted contract, actual costs and applicable law. Nothing here excludes liability or rights that cannot lawfully be excluded.

Misdeclaration may lead to booking cancellation, service refusal, account restrictions, civil claims or regulatory/criminal consequences under applicable law. These outcomes depend on the facts and the competent authority; this page does not determine them in advance.

14. Information handling and reporting concerns

Documents supplied for screening may contain personal, commercial, technical and regulatory information. KRIM handles information under its Privacy Notice and applicable law, and may share relevant details with service providers, customs brokers, insurers, regulators or emergency authorities where necessary and lawful.

Report a suspected misdeclaration, unsafe handling or compliance concern through the Contact page. Do not upload passwords, OTPs, card PINs or unrelated sensitive personal information.

15. Contact and related policies

Before requesting a quote for unusual, regulated or potentially hazardous cargo, describe the goods accurately and ask whether an appropriate provider and route are available. Do not dispatch the cargo until written acceptance is received.
