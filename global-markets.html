Global architecture

Shared foundations, configurable markets

A consistent data and workflow model helps teams operate across regions while preserving local context.

01 / GEOGRAPHY

Countries & markets

Represent countries and launchable markets separately so service availability, business rules, and operational readiness can be managed explicitly.

02 / LOCATION

Locations & time

Support origin and destination locations, local time zones, address formats, and market-specific display conventions.

03 / TRANSPORT

Transport modes

Model road, rail, ocean, air, and multimodal movements through shared shipment concepts with mode-specific execution details.

04 / CURRENCY

Multi-currency workflows

Keep quote, invoice, payment, and settlement currencies explicit. Apply exchange rates and rounding rules transparently instead of assuming one global currency.

05 / NETWORK

Local operating network

Connect shippers, carriers, warehouses, brokers, and service providers through market-aware onboarding, capability checks, and assignment workflows.

06 / CONTROL

Governance & audit

Use access controls, tenant isolation, auditable changes, and market-level policy configuration to support accountable operations.

Market model

Four layers, one operating system

Global core

Shared identities, organizations, shipment lifecycle, permissions, audit events, and integration patterns.

Market layer

Currencies, supported services, language and time settings, local rules, and required documents.

Local network

Verified partners and their actual service areas, equipment, capacity, and compliance status.

Physical execution

Pickup, tracking, handoffs, proof of delivery, exceptions, and settlement supported by real operational evidence.

Multi-currency finance

Currency-aware, not currency-assuming

  • Record the currency on every quote, invoice, payment, and settlement.
  • Keep conversion rate, rate source, and timestamp traceable when conversion is used.
  • Separate customer charges, partner payables, fees, taxes, and refunds.
  • Apply local tax and invoicing requirements only after the relevant market rules are verified.

Controlled operations

Regional rules with accountable controls

  • Validate partner identity, operating authority, service capability, and insurance as applicable.
  • Check customs, sanctions, export-control, privacy, and retention obligations for the relevant route and goods.
  • Apply least-privilege access and tenant isolation to customer and partner data.
  • Maintain audit trails and operational exception handling for material changes.

Scale with evidence

Expand market by market. Keep the core consistent.

KRIM's architecture is global-oriented from the start; actual availability should expand only when local partners, compliance, support, and execution workflows are validated.
