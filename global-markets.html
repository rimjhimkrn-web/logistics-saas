Market & route readiness

A country is not a route. A route is not a booking.

KRIM separates technical configuration from commercial availability and operational activation. This prevents a configured country, currency or transport mode from being mistaken for a service that is ready to accept shipments.

STATE 01

Designed

The platform architecture supports a market or capability conceptually. This alone does not mean it is configured or available.

STATE 02

Configured

Country settings, currency, language, compliance rules and workflows have been entered and reviewed for the intended scope.

STATE 03

Partner-validated

Relevant providers, credentials, route coverage, capacity, service scope and commercial terms have been checked.

STATE 04

Operationally active

End-to-end execution, payment, customer support, exception handling and delivery evidence have passed controlled validation.

KRIM public availability rule

A route should be shown as available only when the relevant origin, destination, transport legs, service providers, pricing, regulatory checks and customer-support ownership are confirmed. Until then, the correct next step is an enquiry for review—not an instant booking promise.

Market activation standard

A market goes live only when the whole chain is ready.

A country may be configured in the platform while a particular service or lane remains unavailable. KRIM should only accept a booking when the complete execution chain has been checked.

Route-level availability rule

A route is bookable only when origin pickup, main-carriage or line-haul, destination handling, required customs steps and final delivery are assigned to accountable providers with confirmed scope and terms. If a leg is unconfirmed, the customer must see that it is a request for review—not a confirmed service or price.

Multi-currency finance

Make every amount explainable.

  • Store currency codes with every quote, invoice, payment, refund and partner payable.
  • Record the FX rate, source, timestamp and conversion method whenever conversion occurs.
  • Show customer charges, partner costs, platform fees and taxes as distinct components.
  • Do not display an estimated rate as a confirmed quote or silently change the settlement currency.

Localized compliance

Respect the rules of each movement.

  • Determine requirements using origin, destination, transit points, cargo type and service mode.
  • Validate customs declarations, permits, licences and restricted or dangerous-goods rules where applicable.
  • Apply privacy, data access and retention controls appropriate to the jurisdictions involved.
  • Escalate uncertain regulatory requirements to qualified local professionals; do not imply legal certification.
