# KRIM First-Partner Acquisition Sprint — Ranchi ↔ Jamshedpur
**Prepared:** 16 November 2026  
**Status:** Research and operating playbook only. No outreach or CRM records created.  
**Pilot objective:** Secure one genuinely capable, independently verified road-freight provider for a tightly scoped Ranchi–Jamshedpur pilot.

## 1. Executive operating decision

KRIM should run a founder-led, evidence-first partner acquisition sprint rather than expanding the website or adding speculative route coverage.

**Target:** one provider, one corridor, one service profile, one controlled shipment pilot.

**Pilot corridor:** Ranchi ↔ Jamshedpur, Jharkhand, India. ROAD mode and both endpoint locations are present in KRIM's active market catalogue. This is not a claim that a carrier is already available.

**Initial service profile:** ordinary, non-hazardous, non-temperature-controlled commercial cargo, only in the exact vehicle and weight band a selected provider proves it can serve. Exclude dangerous goods, cold chain, high-value cargo, oversized loads, household relocation, parcel/courier and multi-leg freight from this first pilot unless the chosen provider's verified capability and separate operating controls explicitly cover them.

**Commercial model:** customer contracts with and pays the carrier directly. KRIM does not receive or settle freight charges. A separately documented KRIM referral/coordination fee is only charged when its contractual trigger is met and after CA/legal review of GST, invoicing, TDS and intermediary treatment.

## 4. First discovery message (draft only)

Subject: Exploring a verified road-freight partnership on Ranchi–Jamshedpur

Hello,

I’m building KRIM Logistics, a logistics operating platform that connects shippers with independently verified transport providers.

We are assessing a small, controlled road-freight pilot between Ranchi and Jamshedpur. We are speaking with established providers to understand their actual operating lanes, vehicle availability, cargo limits, service standards and interest in a direct shipper-provider model.

KRIM does not promise loads or ask a provider to pay for an unverified lead. The provider would contract directly with the shipper and collect freight charges directly. Any separate KRIM referral or coordination fee would be discussed transparently in writing before a transaction.

Would the appropriate operations or business-development representative be available for a 15-minute introductory discussion?

We would initially like to understand:
- whether you serve both directions on this corridor;
- the vehicle types and cargo profiles you can accept;
- whether capacity is owned, leased or subcontracted;
- your documentation, insurance, tracking and proof-of-delivery process;
- your process for evaluating a new technology/referral partner.

Thank you,  
KRIM Logistics  
Global Logistics Operating System

**Do not send this message without explicit founder approval.** Do not attach customer data or request sensitive identity documents over ordinary email or messaging.

## 5. Fifteen-minute discovery call

### A. Company and authority (3 minutes)
1. What is the exact legal name and legal constitution of the operating provider?
2. Who is authorised to discuss a partnership and sign an agreement?
3. Which GST, registration, transport permits and operating licences apply to this service?
4. Is the Ranchi office a company branch, franchise, booking agent or independent contractor?

### B. Lane and actual operations (4 minutes)
5. Do you currently move freight Ranchi → Jamshedpur and Jamshedpur → Ranchi?
6. How many completed loads on this lane in the last 30/90 days? Can you provide non-confidential, redacted evidence?
7. What are the actual transit time, departure schedule, loading cut-off, service days and exceptions?
8. Which cargo types, dimensions, weights and values are excluded?
9. Which exact vehicle classes can you supply, and what is the process for confirming a particular vehicle?

### C. Capacity, safety and liability (4 minutes)
10. Which vehicles are owned, leased or subcontracted? Who controls dispatch and driver assignment?
11. What vehicle registration, fitness, permit, pollution and insurance evidence can be validated?
12. What driver verification, training, safety and incident escalation controls exist?
13. What cargo/transit liability and claims process applies?
14. Can you provide tracking milestones and independently verifiable delivery proof?

### D. Commercial and platform fit (4 minutes)
15. Can the shipper contract with and pay your company directly?
16. Can you provide a written quote with validity, inclusions, exclusions, taxes, cancellation and detention terms?
17. Would you consider a transparent, separate referral/coordination agreement with KRIM?
18. Who handles customer service, complaints, delays, loss, damage and refunds?
19. Are you willing to complete a no-fee verification application and a controlled staging walkthrough?

## 6. Evidence checklist — collect only through KRIM's private workflow

### Legal identity and authority
- Certificate of incorporation / registration or other applicable business proof.
- Current MCA status for a company/LLP, where applicable.
- PAN and GST registration where applicable; verify through official portals.
- Registered and operating addresses.
- Authorised signatory proof and board/authorisation evidence where relevant.
- Bank-account proof only if legally and operationally necessary for a future KRIM fee payment; do not collect it for initial discovery.

### Transport and fleet
- Applicable state/national permits.
- Vehicle registration, fitness, PUC and insurance, checked for expiry.
- Vehicle list and type/capacity with owned/leased/subcontracted distinction.
- Driver licence and applicable credentials.
- Subcontractor due diligence and flow-down obligations.
- Cargo exclusions, loading/unloading responsibilities and safety process.

### Operational proof
- Redacted evidence of recent loads on the exact corridor.
- Sample route plan, transit SLA and dispatch contact.
- Tracking capability and exception escalation.
- Sample POD with personal/customer information redacted.
- Claims, complaints and incident-handling process.
- Customer references, obtained with permission.

### Commercial
- Written rate card or shipment-specific quote.
- Validity and surcharge rules.
- Cancellation, detention, waiting, toll, loading and unloading treatment.
- Customer contract and liability terms.
- KRIM partner/referral agreement, signed by an authorised person.
- Fee trigger, invoice and payment evidence; no fee for mere lead submission.

## 7. Qualification scorecard

Score each criterion 0–5; evidence is mandatory for critical gates. Scores prioritise follow-up; they do not override legal or safety approval.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Exact corridor proof and recent completed loads | 20 |
| Legal identity, signatory and regulatory evidence | 20 |
| Vehicle/fleet control and route capacity | 15 |
| Insurance, safety and claims controls | 15 |
| Tracking, POD and operational responsiveness | 10 |
| Written pricing and commercial transparency | 10 |
| Direct-contract model and willingness to partner | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Decision bands:
- **80–100:** priority for formal due diligence, if every mandatory gate passes.
- **65–79:** continue discovery; do not activate.
- **Below 65:** hold or decline.
- **Automatic hold regardless of score:** identity mismatch, expired/invalid mandatory permits or insurance, undisclosed subcontracting, no route evidence, no authorised signatory, refusal of written liability terms, or request to route customer freight money through KRIM.

## 8. Acquisition pipeline and data discipline

Use these stages consistently:
1. Research lead — public source only; no relationship implied.
2. Contact approved — founder explicitly authorises a specific contact and channel.
3. Contact attempted — record date, channel and result.
4. Discovery scheduled — named authorised contact and meeting time.
5. Discovery completed — factual notes and next action.
6. Qualified for due diligence — lane/service fit appears plausible; not approved.
7. Verification submitted — partner uses authenticated onboarding.
8. Legal identity approved — separate human review.
9. Compliance approved — all required evidence valid.
10. Capability approved — exact mode, equipment, cargo and weight scope.
11. Service area approved — exact route locations and evidence.
12. Contract executed — correct parties, signatory, term and fee scope.
13. Capacity test passed — actual availability confirmation process tested.
14. Pilot eligible — all preceding gates complete.
15. Pilot completed — shipment, exception and POD evidence reviewed.
16. Partner active — only the approved scope is exposed to matching.
17. Rejected / dormant — reason, date and re-entry conditions recorded.

Do not equate contact, interest, a signed NDA, registration, capability submission or a directory listing with partner approval.

### Minimum internal prospect record (design recommendation)
- Stable prospect ID and legal-name candidate.
- Source URL, source date and evidence confidence.
- Exact public business contact route and contact permission basis.
- Prospect owner and next-action due date.
- Lane and service hypotheses, each labelled unverified.
- Discovery history, consent/legal basis, opt-out/suppression status.
- Legal identity, compliance, capability, service-area and contract review references.
- Reviewer, decision, rationale, evidence expiry and audit event.
- No sensitive documents in prospect notes; private evidence belongs in the restricted verification workflow.

**Implementation rule:** do not insert these public prospects into production `krim_partner_opportunities`; that table is not a prospect CRM. The next safe engineering step is a schema-independent UX/API specification and staging-only implementation plan for a dedicated partner prospect record, immutable interaction history, owner assignment, due dates and conversion linkage to the existing verification case. Before production deployment, recover and reconcile the migration history and test tenant isolation.

## 10. Sprint plan

### Sprint 0 — founder approval
- Choose one or two specific prospects to approach.
- Approve the exact contact person/channel/message.
- Decide whether discovery is a no-fee, no-guaranteed-load conversation.
- Confirm no customer details will be shared without consent.

### Sprint 1 — discovery
- Conduct two or three short conversations.
- Record actual route, cargo, fleet, capacity and operating constraints.
- Reject mismatched providers early.

### Sprint 2 — evidence
- Invite one candidate to the registered-business pathway.
- Collect legal/operational evidence privately.
- Independently review identity, compliance, capability and service area.

### Sprint 3 — contract and controlled pilot
- Obtain professional review of customer/provider and KRIM referral terms.
- Verify route and shipment-specific capacity.
- Run staging end-to-end tests for quote, acceptance, dispatch, tracking, exception and POD.
- Conduct one low-risk shipment only when all gates pass.

### Sprint success measures
- 2–3 discovery conversations completed.
- At least 1 candidate submits complete verification evidence.
- At least 1 candidate proves recent lane activity.
- At least 1 exact capability and service area independently approved.
- Signed contract and tested direct-payment model.
- First pilot delivered with verified POD and no unresolved critical exception.

If no provider passes, the correct result is **no launch on this corridor**, not lowering verification standards.
