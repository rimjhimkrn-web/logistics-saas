# KRIM First 10 Business Design Partners
Date: 11 November 2026
Status: acquisition and product-validation playbook; not a public price list or paid offer.

## Goal
Recruit 10 qualified logistics businesses to validate one repeatable software problem, product usage and willingness to pay. The purpose is to establish evidence for a paid SaaS offer—not to count casual registrations as demand.

## Initial ideal customer profile
Prioritise owner-led or operations-led businesses that manage recurring shipment enquiries, partner coordination, quotes, documents and customer updates:
- Freight forwarders and logistics service providers.
- Regional road transport operators with multiple customers or subcontractors.
- Warehouse operators that need receiving, QC, inventory and dispatch visibility.

Avoid broad mass marketing until one segment shows repeated usage and a clear buying trigger.

## First product hypothesis
KRIM helps a logistics business manage partner/customer enquiries, quote workflows, shipment visibility, operational documents and team handoffs in one controlled workspace.

Do not promise carrier coverage, live rates, delivery performance, customs capability or integrations unless those capabilities are independently verified for the relevant route and account.

## Discovery questions
Ask prospects to describe their current workflow before showing features:
1. How many shipment enquiries or jobs do you handle in a typical month?
2. Which tools currently hold enquiries, quotes, documents and shipment updates?
3. Where do delays, duplicate entry or missed customer updates occur?
4. Which workflow costs the team the most time each week?
5. Who decides whether to buy operational software, and what is the approval process?
6. What would need to improve for the software to be worth paying for?
7. What budget range is already allocated to comparable software?
8. Would the business commit to a structured evaluation and provide weekly feedback?

Record the role, company type, workflow, current tools, pain severity, decision-maker, budget evidence, next action and consent to follow up. Do not collect sensitive shipment/customer data during discovery.

## Pilot design
- Recruit up to 10 organisations from one initial segment.
- Use free, time-limited evaluation access only after terms, privacy and support boundaries are clear.
- Configure each organisation's workspace and train one accountable champion.
- Use test or appropriately authorised operational data; do not imply that unverified routes or partners are available.
- Review usage and blockers weekly.
- At the end, ask the decision-maker to choose: stop, continue free evaluation for a defined reason, or express written willingness to purchase a clearly scoped paid plan after commercial/legal/payment gates are cleared.

## Evidence required before publishing prices
- At least 8 completed discovery interviews in the same segment.
- At least 5 organisations identify the same high-priority recurring problem.
- At least 3 decision-makers confirm a credible budget or willingness-to-pay range.
- A clear feature boundary and support cost estimate.
- No unresolved critical product, privacy, legal, tax or payment issue.

These are internal learning thresholds, not proof of revenue.

## Suggested product packaging to test (not published or activated)
- **Partner Workspace:** enquiries, quote handling, document workflow and basic team access.
- **Operations Pro:** shipment visibility, team permissions, activity history and reporting.
- **Enterprise:** multi-branch administration, audit, integrations and contracted support.
- **Warehouse Operations:** receiving, QC, inventory and dispatch workflow.

Do not sell a feature that is not deployed, tested and supported. Do not charge for marketplace access, freight handling or transaction facilitation under a software label.

## Outreach message
Subject: Exploring a simpler operations workspace for logistics teams

Hello,

I’m building KRIM, a logistics operating platform for businesses that manage shipment enquiries, quotes, partner coordination and operational documents.

I’m speaking with a small group of logistics businesses to understand where current workflows create delays or repeated manual work. I’m not asking you to change your existing systems or commit to a purchase.

Would you be open to a short 20-minute conversation about how your team currently manages these workflows? If there is a fit, I can show you the relevant KRIM workspace and invite your team to a structured evaluation.

Regards,
KRIM Logistics

## Qualification and funnel metrics
Track weekly:
- Named target businesses contacted.
- Decision-maker conversations held.
- Qualified pain points confirmed.
- Evaluation workspaces activated.
- Organisations active in week 2 and week 4.
- Core workflow completion rate.
- Support time per organisation.
- Written willingness-to-pay evidence.
- Paid conversion only after commercial activation is legally and operationally cleared.

Do not report sign-ups, free pilots, gross freight value or uncollected invoices as revenue.

## Commercial conversion gate
No paid SaaS subscription may be offered or charged until:
1. The exact software supply and KRIM's legal/tax position are reviewed.
2. The payment provider confirms merchant and recurring-billing eligibility in writing.
3. Subscription billing, cancellation, refund, invoice and ledger controls pass tests.
4. Customer terms, privacy notice, support scope and pricing are approved.
5. A controlled paid pilot can be reconciled from provider payment through settlement and accounting.

## Definition of a successful first cohort
A successful cohort produces:
- A clearly identified buyer segment and recurring workflow.
- At least 5 businesses completing the core workflow repeatedly.
- At least 3 written, credible willingness-to-pay signals.
- Measured support and operating costs.
- A defined paid offer with a clear buyer, outcome and price hypothesis.

Sustainable revenue is proven later through actual paid conversions, renewals, retention and positive contribution margin over consecutive billing cycles.
