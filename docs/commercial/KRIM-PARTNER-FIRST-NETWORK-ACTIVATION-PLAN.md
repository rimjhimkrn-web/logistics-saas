# KRIM Partner-First Network Activation Plan
Date: 11 November 2026
Status: internal operating policy; not a public availability promise, price list, or authority to activate paid checkout.

## Decision
KRIM launches its service marketplace supply-side first, then activates customer acquisition only in verified service cells. A service cell is the combination of origin area, destination area, transport mode, service type and any material constraints (for example cargo type, vehicle/equipment, capacity and operating dates).

KRIM must never sell a customer a promise of transportation where it has no suitable, currently verified provider. A global-ready website or app does not mean global operational coverage.

## What KRIM sells
KRIM has two distinct products and revenue paths:

### 1. KRIM Operations Software (B2B SaaS)
Sell real software capabilities to logistics businesses that can use KRIM with their own customers, staff, vendors and operations—even if KRIM has not yet sourced them a new marketplace customer. Examples, only when deployed and supported:
- enquiry and quotation workflow;
- shipment and job records;
- document and proof-of-delivery workflows;
- team roles, permissions and activity history;
- warehouse receiving, QC, inventory and dispatch;
- operational reports, integrations and enterprise controls.

The buyer pays for the software capability and agreed support, not for a promise of KRIM-generated leads. Start with a free, time-limited evaluation/design-partner cohort. Do not charge until the feature is live, useful, supported, contractually described and legal/tax/payment gates are cleared.

### 2. KRIM Marketplace / Coordination
Customers submit service requirements; suitable partners decide whether to quote and fulfil. Customer registration and an initial service enquiry should be free. Do not charge a customer a recurring marketplace-access subscription merely to discover that no provider is available. Any future booking/coordination fee or commission requires a separately reviewed contract, tax treatment, refund policy and payment flow. Never disguise freight or transaction consideration as SaaS.

## Supply-first activation sequence

### Stage A — Build a qualified partner pipeline
Recruit a small, manageable cohort rather than trying to cover every route:
- regional road transport operators and fleet owners;
- freight forwarders and multimodal providers;
- warehouses and fulfilment operators;
- air/ocean/customs providers only where the relevant authority, capability and service chain can be verified.

Collect business identity, authorised contact, service modes, actual origin/destination coverage, cargo restrictions, capacity/equipment, operating hours, service terms, insurance/licence evidence where applicable, escalation contact and consent to verification. Do not mark a provider active solely because it completed a registration form.

### Stage B — Verify and test
For each partner:
1. Verify business identity and authorised representative.
2. Review required licences, permits, insurance and supporting documents for the service offered.
3. Validate each claimed service cell and its constraints.
4. Confirm quotation response process, validity, exclusions, cancellation and claims escalation.
5. Run a controlled test enquiry/quote and confirm the partner can respond.
6. Approve only the specific capabilities supported by evidence; keep other capabilities pending or rejected.
7. Set an evidence review/expiry date and suspend stale or failed capabilities.

No fabricated rates, availability, transit times, tracking events or service claims.

### Stage C — Open a limited customer pilot
Open customer acquisition only for service cells with at least one approved partner whose capability and current operating status are confirmed. Prefer more than one qualified option where feasible, but do not invent a second provider or imply redundancy when only one exists.

Before accepting a live shipment, reconfirm capacity, price, schedule, cargo fit and the partner's acceptance for that specific job. A capability approval is not a guaranteed vehicle, slot or price.

### Stage D — Handle uncovered requirements honestly
If no suitable partner is found:
- tell the customer that coverage is not currently confirmed;
- do not show a quote, price, ETA or availability badge;
- offer a no-obligation coverage review / waitlist request;
- record only the minimum route and service details needed, with privacy notice and contact consent;
- route the request to KRIM operations for partner sourcing;
- tell the customer that submission is not a booking and does not guarantee a provider;
- update the customer only when a real provider is verified and has confirmed interest/capacity.

Do not charge a marketplace access, booking or coordination fee for an unfulfilled uncovered request. If a customer separately buys genuinely useful KRIM software, its subscription must not be represented as payment for unavailable transport.

## Customer charging policy
- Customer registration: free.
- Uncovered route/service enquiry and coverage-review request: free.
- Quote comparison and booking: no charge by default during initial pilot; any later fee must be separately approved, disclosed and legally reviewed.
- Customer SaaS: optional and only for distinct, deployed business software features that deliver value even when a particular route has no marketplace provider. Do not make it a prerequisite for submitting a basic service enquiry.
- Freight charges: quoted and contracted transparently with the actual service provider or under a separately reviewed KRIM commercial model. Do not collect freight funds until the legal role, payment provider approval, invoicing, refunds, settlement and accounting are ready.

## Partner charging policy
- Initial registration, verification and controlled design-partner onboarding: free during the pilot.
- Do not promise lead volume, exclusive routes, guaranteed loads or revenue.
- A future partner SaaS plan may charge for proven workflow software (for example quote management, team permissions, documents and reports), whether the partner handles KRIM marketplace enquiries or its own customers.
- Paid plans require an actual feature checklist, support boundary, contract, cancellation/refund terms, tax review, approved recurring payment method and tested entitlement/billing controls.
- Marketplace commission, referral fee and SaaS subscription must remain distinct in contracts, invoices and accounting.

## Initial launch gates and measures
Do not declare a service cell open until all are true:
- at least one partner is approved for the exact service cell;
- required evidence is reviewed and in date;
- the partner has confirmed how it accepts and responds to live requests;
- operations has a working escalation path;
- customer-facing wording accurately describes the level of availability;
- the end-to-end enquiry → partner response → customer decision → assignment process has passed a controlled test.

Track:
- partners contacted, applications, verified partners and approved service cells;
- quote response rate and response time;
- confirmed capacity and actual accepted assignments;
- fulfilment, cancellation, claims and complaint rates;
- uncovered demand by origin/destination/mode;
- customer repeat use and partner retention;
- software activation, repeated feature use, support cost and paid conversion.

Do not count registrations, unverified coverage, free pilots, enquiries, quotations or gross freight value as KRIM revenue.

## First practical rollout
1. Choose one narrow launch segment and a small set of service cells around actual partner capability; do not assume a nationwide or global footprint.
2. Manually contact and qualify the first partner cohort.
3. Verify and test the partners and service cells.
4. Open a controlled customer pilot only for those cells; accept uncovered requirements as free coverage-review leads.
5. Separately invite a small group of logistics businesses to test KRIM Operations Software against their own real workflows.
6. Review usage, service quality, support cost and willingness to pay before publishing prices or enabling paid subscriptions.

## Non-negotiable customer promise
KRIM will show the difference between:
- **Verified capability:** a provider passed the relevant capability review;
- **Request submitted:** the customer has asked for a service;
- **Quote received:** a provider has made a specific offer;
- **Booking confirmed:** the provider has explicitly accepted the specific job;
- **In transit / delivered:** only verified operational events support these statuses.

These states must never be conflated in product UI, marketing, reports or customer communications.

## Legal, tax and payment boundary
This plan is an operating design, not a legal or tax opinion. Before paid commercial activation, obtain a review of KRIM's exact role (software supplier, marketplace operator, agent/principal or other), Indian GST and income-tax treatment, consumer and data-protection obligations, contracts, payment-provider eligibility, refunds, settlements and cross-border requirements. A software-first launch, foreign customer or foreign payment currency does not by itself remove Indian obligations. Keep paid checkout disabled wherever the applicable legal or merchant-eligibility gate is unresolved.
