# KRIM Global Logistics OS — Legal, Regulatory & Compliance Control Framework

Version: 1.0  
Review date: 4 November 2026  
Initial launch jurisdiction: India (Jharkhand/Ranchi base; operations may span other Indian States)  
Long-term scope: Multi-country, multimodal logistics marketplace and operating system  
Status: Design and due-diligence framework — NOT a legal opinion, licence, registration or compliance certificate.

## 1. Non-negotiable operating principle

KRIM must not accept, price, book, carry, broker, clear, store, insure, pay out or market a regulated service in a jurisdiction until its legal role, entity eligibility, registrations, permits, contracts, insurance, tax treatment, data controls and operational capability have been assessed and approved for that activity.

A technology platform, partner-led model, payment-gateway integration, GST registration, Udyam registration, or a partner's licence does not automatically authorize KRIM itself to act as a carrier, common carrier, freight forwarder, customs broker, payment aggregator, insurer, warehouse operator or e-commerce marketplace.

The applicable legal duties depend on what KRIM actually does: who contracts with the customer, who sets/collects the price, who issues transport documents, who takes custody/control, who arranges carriage, who receives funds, who bears liability, and who controls the marketplace.

No checklist can guarantee that a business will never make a legal mistake. The control objective is to maintain a verified, versioned legal register, obtain qualified local advice, monitor changes, preserve evidence and block activity when a required approval is missing.

## 2. Critical India legal-structure gate — resolve before commercial marketplace launch

### 2.1 Marketplace / e-commerce entity classification

Obtain a written Indian legal opinion on whether KRIM's intended customer-facing model is an e-commerce entity, marketplace e-commerce entity, service marketplace, intermediary, logistics service provider, agent/broker or principal supplier under each relevant statute and rule.

Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 Rule 4(1)(a) specifies eligible legal forms for an e-commerce entity, including an Indian company incorporated under the Companies Acts, specified foreign-company structures, or a qualifying office/branch/agency outside India owned or controlled by an Indian resident. The Government's 2020 parliamentary explanation describes this requirement. KRIM is presently structured as a sole proprietorship. **Do not assume a sole proprietorship satisfies Rule 4(1)(a) if KRIM is classified as an e-commerce entity.** Obtain a written opinion on applicability and appropriate entity structure before operating a marketplace within the Rules. If incorporation is required, compare a private limited company and other legally available structures with a CA/CS and lawyer, including costs, governance, tax and ownership. Do not make a cosmetic entity change without checking all applicable laws.

The 2026 E-Commerce Amendment Rules take effect on 1 January 2027. Prepare the product and policies for:
- Prominent legal name, principal geographic address, website and customer-care/grievance-officer contact details.
- Grievance receipt acknowledgement within 48 hours, copy of complaint as recorded, and redressal within one month.
- Participation in the National Consumer Helpline convergence process.
- Seller/service-provider disclosures and post-purchase identity access for dispute resolution.
- Clear disclosure of ranking parameters and their relative importance.
- No misleading manipulation of search results or indexes.
- Clear identification of sponsored listings.
- Prior-price disclosure when announcing a reduction (lowest price in the preceding 30 days).
- Seller name in invoices at the same font size as marketplace entity name.
- Compliance with Dark Patterns Guidelines, 2023 and an annual self-audit with a prominently displayed certificate.
- Express affirmative consent for specified marketplace uses of consumer information and restrictions on unrelated bundled fees.
- Relevant delivery, shipment, payment, return, refund and grievance information.

Map these controls to KRIM's actual service marketplace model; do not assume every provision applies identically to every logistics transaction.

### 2.2 Road common-carrier / broker / booking-company classification

The Carriage by Road Act, 2007 definition of common carrier expressly includes persons collecting, storing, forwarding or distributing goods for road carriage and includes a goods booking company, contractor, agent and broker in the described circumstances. Section 3 prohibits engaging in common-carrier business without registration. Therefore, a partner-led model does not automatically remove KRIM from scope. Obtain written counsel advice on KRIM's proposed role, routes and contracts, and secure any required registration before relevant road activity. Map goods forwarding notes, goods receipts, declarations of value, liability limits, claims and annual returns.

### 2.3 Other Indian business and local registrations

Maintain an entity-specific checklist for:
- PAN, bank/current account, accounting books, beneficial ownership and authorized signatories.
- GST registration, place-of-business evidence, tax classification, place/time of supply, invoicing, input tax credit, returns, e-invoicing/e-way bills where applicable, TDS/TCS and marketplace reporting.
- Udyam/MSME status and accuracy of enterprise details; it is not a substitute for other licences.
- TAN and tax deduction obligations where applicable.
- Jharkhand and relevant municipal/local trade, Shops and Establishments, professional tax if applicable, signage, fire, building/occupancy and other local registrations as triggered by premises and staffing.
- Employment, contractor, driver and workplace obligations, including applicable central/state rules and registrations.
- Registered office, principal place of business, virtual-office/consent and address-document requirements. Never represent a virtual address as a valid registered or GST business address until the relevant authority's requirements and provider documents are satisfied.

### 2.4 GST and marketplace tax design

Determine whether KRIM is an electronic commerce operator (ECO), whether it collects consideration, whether section 52 TCS applies, whether section 9(5) notified services apply, whether suppliers require registration, and how transport/freight/warehousing/agency/commission supplies are classified. CBIC explains that ECO registration and supplier registration/TCS rules depend on statutory conditions and notified services; do not hard-code a generic 1% on every booking assumption. Build separate tax treatment for principal supply, disclosed agency, commission, partner supply, reimbursements, import/export and inter-State supply. Obtain a written CA tax memorandum and current notification review before production invoicing.

### 2.5 Road vehicle and carrier partner controls

For every road carrier/vehicle, verify applicable:
- Motor Vehicles Act permits and goods-carriage permit conditions.
- Registration certificate, fitness certificate, pollution certificate, road tax, national/state permit and authorization for the intended route.
- Driver licence class, endorsements, training, identity, working-time/safety controls and applicable employment/contractor requirements.
- Motor third-party insurance and other required insurance.
- Vehicle ownership/lease/authorization, load limits, axle/weight limits, route restrictions, state border/toll requirements and hazardous cargo authorizations.
- Common-carrier registration where required.
- Contract, consignment note/goods receipt, proof of delivery, claims and incident reporting.

### 2.6 Import/export, customs and trade controls

For India cross-border activity, determine whether KRIM is acting as exporter/importer, customs broker, freight forwarder, carrier, authorized representative or technology provider. Do not make customs declarations or act as a customs broker without required authorization and competent personnel.
- Verify IEC requirement and exemptions for the actual importer/exporter and activity.
- Map DGFT Foreign Trade Policy, ITC(HS) classification, prohibited/restricted/canalized goods, licensing, sanctions and product-specific NOCs.
- Map Customs Act, valuation, classification, origin, duties, shipping bills, bills of entry, manifests, bonds, drawback/incentives and record retention.
- Verify ICEGATE access/authorization and customs-broker licence requirements when acting in that capacity.
- Preserve commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, shipping bill/bill of export, bill of entry, certificates of origin, permits, inspection and product certificates as applicable.
- Screen restricted goods, dual-use items, sanctioned parties, destination, end-use and diversion risks.
- Apply additional controls for food, pharmaceuticals, medical devices, chemicals, batteries, waste, wildlife products, alcohol, tobacco, radio equipment and other regulated cargo.

### 2.7 Payments, foreign exchange and funds custody

Razorpay or another authorized provider's onboarding does not itself authorize KRIM to operate a payment system or payment aggregator. Obtain specialist advice on whether KRIM merely uses a payment gateway/authorized payment aggregator or itself receives, pools, controls, routes, holds or settles customer money. Do not maintain an informal escrow, wallet, stored-value balance or pooled funds arrangement.
- Review RBI Payment Aggregator Directions 2025, Payment and Settlement Systems Act, applicable RBI rules and provider contracts.
- Review FEMA, RBI directions, authorized-dealer bank requirements, purpose codes, export/import realization, refunds, cross-border collection, foreign currency conversion and repatriation.
- Use provider-approved settlement/split mechanisms only; verify legal eligibility and actual provider support for the entity and business model.
- Separate customer funds, KRIM revenue, taxes, partner payable, refunds, chargebacks and reserves in the ledger.
- Require idempotent webhooks, signature verification, reconciliation, maker-checker approvals, refund controls and immutable audit.
- No foreign-currency payment, FX quote or partner payout until the payment rail, FX source, bank route, tax and FEMA treatment are approved for the country corridor.

### 2.8 Privacy, cybersecurity and electronic records in India

Maintain a data inventory and lawful-purpose map for customers, partners, drivers, employees, contacts, shipment geolocation, identity documents, licence details, financial records, support records and AI conversations.
- Map the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025 using their notified phased commencement schedule; do not state that every substantive obligation commenced on the notification date. Track the relevant commencement dates, including the 12-month and 18-month phases.
- Until repealed provisions actually commence, assess continuing Information Technology Act/SPDI obligations and applicable rules.
- Implement clear notices, purpose limitation, minimization, retention/deletion, consent where required, rights/grievance workflows, processor contracts, security safeguards, breach response and children's data restrictions.
- Assess cross-border data transfers, localization/sectoral restrictions, subprocessors and international support access.
- Review CERT-In directions, incident reporting timelines, log retention, time synchronization, evidence preservation and designated points of contact.
- Implement secure electronic contracts, electronic signatures, reliable audit trails, evidence authenticity and retention under applicable IT/evidence laws.
- Do not collect Aadhaar or other sensitive identifiers merely because a form can accept them; establish lawful basis, necessity, security and approved verification method first.
- Establish AI data handling rules: no training on customer/partner confidential data without lawful basis and appropriate agreement; role-based retrieval; human review; provenance; explainability for consequential decisions; prompt-injection defenses; tool allowlists; and audit of any action.

### 2.9 Labour, safety, premises and environment

The four Labour Codes were announced as effective from 21 November 2025; assess applicable central rules, Jharkhand rules, notifications and transition arrangements with local counsel. Map wages, social security, EPF/ESI where applicable, contract labour, gig/platform worker rules, occupational health and safety, working hours, leave, accident reporting, workplace harassment prevention and contractor due diligence. Determine obligations based on whether KRIM employs, contracts, controls or merely connects workers.

For warehouses/offices/depots, assess fire NOC, building use, occupancy, electrical safety, hazardous storage, pollution-control consent, waste/e-waste/battery handling, local trade permissions and environmental permits as applicable.

## 3. Mode-specific global transport and cargo compliance

### Road
Country-specific carrier/broker/forwarder registration, vehicle permits, operator licensing, driver qualifications, insurance, roadworthiness, weight/dimension rules, cabotage and cross-border permits. International conventions apply only where the relevant states, route and carriage are within scope (for example CMR, TIR, ADR and related UNECE instruments). Dangerous goods require correct classification, packaging, marking, placarding, training, vehicle equipment and emergency response.

### Rail
Railway operator access, freight booking and terminal rules, wagon/container acceptance, dangerous goods rules, CIM/SMGS or regional carriage regimes where applicable, customs transit and liability conventions.

### Ocean / inland waterways
Carrier/forwarder/NVOCC/ship agent and port/terminal licences where required by jurisdiction. Map SOLAS, verified gross mass, IMDG dangerous goods, MARPOL pollution controls, ISPS/security, port-state/coastal rules, sanctions, bills of lading, cargo claims and applicable carriage conventions. Verify the current mandatory edition and amendment dates of each technical code before configuring cargo acceptance.

### Air
Airline/air cargo agent/forwarder and regulated-agent/security approvals as required. Map ICAO Technical Instructions, IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, cargo security screening, known-consignor/regulated-agent rules, aviation security, air waybill, customs and liability convention requirements. Dangerous goods acceptance, training and documentation must be enforced before quoting or booking restricted cargo.

### Cargo eligibility and product restrictions
Create a shipment-level compliance engine for cargo description, HS code, origin/destination, end-user/end-use, value, weight, dimensions, dangerous goods class/UN number, temperature, perishability, controlled substances, sanctions and required permits. Unknown or incomplete data must fail closed for restricted/high-risk cargo. Provide manual compliance review and preserve decisions/evidence.

## 4. Global country-by-country legal workstream

Do not use a single global compliance checkbox. Create a jurisdiction/corridor matrix for each country of establishment, customer, origin, destination, transit, storage, carrier, payment and data processing.

Each country pack must record:
1. Entity eligibility, foreign ownership, local establishment, tax residence and permanent-establishment risk.
2. Marketplace/intermediary/agency/principal/carrier/forwarder/customs-broker classification.
3. Business licences, transport registrations, permits, professional licences and local representatives.
4. Consumer, e-commerce, advertising, ranking, disclosure, dark-pattern, complaint and refund rules.
5. GST/VAT/sales tax, withholding, invoicing, e-invoice, customs duties, tax nexus and transfer pricing.
6. Customs, import/export, sanctions, export controls, product restrictions, origin and trade documentation.
7. Payment licensing, payment provider eligibility, currency controls, FX, repatriation and settlement.
8. Privacy, data protection, cross-border transfer, data residency, breach reporting and retention.
9. Cybersecurity, critical infrastructure, incident reporting and sectoral security obligations.
10. Labour, contractor/gig worker, immigration, occupational safety and social security.
11. Competition/antitrust, marketplace neutrality, self-preferencing, pricing, exclusivity and information barriers.
12. Insurance, liability, cargo claims, limitation periods and compulsory cover.
13. Accessibility, language, electronic signatures, consumer records and digital evidence.
14. Environmental, emissions, waste, packaging, battery and dangerous-goods controls.
15. Dispute resolution, governing law, local courts, arbitration and enforceability.
16. Local counsel sign-off, accountable owner, source links, effective date, last reviewed date and next review date.

Initial international legal domains to evaluate when expansion is chosen include:
- EU/EEA: GDPR, consumer/e-commerce rules, Digital Services Act where applicable, VAT/customs, competition, accessibility, sanctions/export controls, transport and dangerous-goods regimes.
- United Kingdom: UK GDPR/data protection, consumer/e-commerce, VAT/customs, competition, sanctions and transport licensing.
- United States: federal/state privacy and consumer rules, sales tax nexus, customs/export controls/sanctions, FMCSA broker/carrier requirements, state transport and employment rules.
- Other markets: local equivalents and country-specific foreign ownership, payment, customs, data, transport and labour controls.

This list is a starting map, not a complete statement of law in any country. Do not activate a country because it appears in the database catalogue.

## 5. Contracts, liability, insurance and disputes

Before the first commercial shipment, have counsel approve and version:
- Customer/shipper master terms and shipment-specific booking confirmation.
- Partner/carrier, freight-forwarder, customs broker, warehouse, driver and subcontractor agreements.
- Enterprise master services agreement, statement of work, SLA and service credits.
- Agency/principal disclosure and who contracts with whom for each transaction.
- Rate validity, surcharges, accessorials, taxes, FX, quote expiry and price-change controls.
- Cargo description, prohibited goods, shipper warranties, packaging, dangerous-goods declarations and inspection rights.
- Custody, handover, tracking, delay, loss, damage, temperature excursion, force majeure and claims procedure.
- Liability limits, declared value, higher-risk rates, indemnity, subrogation and limitation periods consistent with mandatory law.
- Payment collection, settlement timing, reserves, chargebacks, refunds, tax and reconciliation.
- Privacy/data processing, confidentiality, security, incident notification, audit and subcontractor flow-down.
- IP, acceptable use, suspension, termination, records, governing law, courts/arbitration and notices.

Insurance programme to be assessed with a licensed broker and counsel:
- Cargo/marine cargo (shipper's interest) cover.
- Carrier's legal liability / motor third-party and relevant vehicle cover.
- Freight forwarder/logistics operator liability and professional indemnity.
- Warehouseman's liability, property, fire and stock cover.
- General/public liability, employer/work injury cover where required, cyber/privacy and crime/fidelity cover.
- Confirm policy territory, modes, cargo exclusions, declared values, subcontractor coverage, limits, deductibles, claims notice and insurer solvency. Partner declarations alone are not proof of cover.

## 6. Marketplace trust, fair dealing and competition

- Accurate legal identity, service scope, partner identity and verification status.
- No fabricated reviews, ratings, coverage, capacity, prices, tracking or delivery promises.
- Explain quote composition, taxes, surcharges, cancellation/refund and ranking factors.
- Clearly label sponsored placements and commercial relationships.
- Maintain fair, auditable ranking and prevent discriminatory or deceptive manipulation.
- Separate paid promotion from verification and operational eligibility.
- Prevent misuse of customer data, self-preferencing and unauthorized cross-partner disclosure.
- Maintain complaint, escalation, appeal, claims, chargeback and dispute workflows.
- Consent-based marketing and communication preferences, unsubscribe/opt-out and channel-specific rules.

## 7. Legal and compliance controls to build into the product

Create first-class, auditable records for:
- Jurisdictions and regulatory requirements with source, version, effective dates, applicability logic, owner and review status.
- Entity legal profiles and entity/branch/establishment registrations.
- Licences/permits with issuing authority, number, territory, scope, expiry, status, evidence and renewal reminders.
- Partner compliance profiles and per-service/per-route eligibility.
- Cargo restrictions, sanctions-screening cases, export-control reviews and manual release approvals.
- Tax profiles, tax determination snapshots and invoice evidence.
- Insurance policies and verified coverage/expiry.
- Contract templates, signed agreements, versions, signatories and acceptance evidence.
- Privacy notices, consent records, data-subject requests, retention schedules and processor register.
- Regulatory incidents, complaints, claims, audit findings and corrective actions.
- Market launch approvals with legal, tax, finance, operations, security and executive sign-offs.

Enforcement rules:
- No expired licence or insurance may qualify a provider for a new assignment.
- No restricted cargo may proceed without required permits and authorized human release.
- No cross-border route may proceed without origin, transit and destination checks.
- No payment or payout may proceed outside the approved provider, currency, country and settlement configuration.
- No market may activate with missing mandatory legal, tax, insurance, privacy, customer support or operational approvals.
- Keep immutable evidence of each approval and every change to legal status.

## 8. India-first launch gates — mandatory before commercial launch

### Gate A — legal form and role
- Written opinion on Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules applicability and entity form.
- Written opinion on Carriage by Road Act/common-carrier registration and KRIM's actual road brokerage/booking role.
- Written opinion on customs-broker, freight-forwarder, payment aggregation, agency/principal and funds-custody boundaries.
- Entity structure, registered address and local registrations confirmed.

### Gate B — tax and payments
- GST classification and ECO/TCS/section 9(5) memorandum; correct invoices and returns.
- Razorpay merchant account and settlement model approved for the actual entity and service.
- Payment, refund, chargeback, partner settlement and ledger reconciliation tested with controlled real-money amounts.
- FEMA/authorized-dealer controls before any foreign-currency or cross-border collection/payout.

### Gate C — people, partners and assets
- Each carrier/warehouse/customs/forwarder partner's legal identity, licence, permit, insurance, bank account, tax identity and authorized signatory verified.
- Driver identity/licence, vehicle permit/fitness/insurance and employment/contractor controls verified.
- Facility permissions, fire/safety, hazardous goods and inventory controls verified where applicable.
- Executed agreements, privacy/security obligations and liability allocation.

### Gate D — customers and consumer protection
- Customer terms, pricing, service scope, refund/cancellation, privacy, grievance and claims approved.
- Grievance officer and complaint SLAs implemented.
- Marketplace legal identity and provider disclosures visible.
- Ranking, sponsored placements, dark patterns, provider disclosure and invoice requirements ready for 1 January 2027 amendments.
- Marketing consent, communication opt-out and complaint evidence.

### Gate E — operational safety and security
- Real route/mode/provider coverage verified; catalogue records are not treated as coverage.
- Cargo restrictions, dangerous goods, sanctions and export controls tested.
- RLS/RBAC, MFA, admin AAL2, secrets, webhook signatures, rate limits and incident response reviewed.
- Backups, restore test, disaster recovery, monitoring, on-call ownership and support escalation proven.
- Mobile/browser accessibility, localization, failure/retry, duplicate-event, reconciliation and role-isolation tests pass.

### Gate F — controlled pilot
- Start with a deliberately narrow India corridor, defined commodity scope, one or a few verified providers, and capped transaction values.
- Run a non-commercial sandbox test first; then a small, explicitly approved paid pilot.
- Reconcile every shipment, invoice, tax entry, payment, refund, partner payable and delivery evidence.
- Review incidents, claims, customer complaints, service quality and unit economics before widening geography, cargo or transport modes.

## 9. Compliance governance and change monitoring

- Assign a named accountable compliance owner; obtain retained Indian counsel, CA/CS and insurance broker input.
- Maintain a regulatory source register using official Gazette, India Code, ministry, regulator and treaty-body sources.
- Review high-impact changes monthly and before each market/mode/cargo launch; run a quarterly full register review.
- Record legal interpretation, applicability, evidence, accountable owner, due date, effective date and approval.
- Require two-person approval for market activation, sanctions release, licence override, payout exceptions and high-risk cargo.
- Preserve a documented legal hold, complaint, incident, breach and claims process.
- Re-run impact assessment whenever KRIM changes legal entity, contracts, custody of goods, funds flow, partner role, customer location, route, cargo type, data processor or country.

## 11. No-false-certification rule

KRIM must never display legally compliant globally, licensed, insured, verified, approved or operational unless the exact entity, jurisdiction, service, route, cargo, period and evidence support that claim. Global-ready architecture and globally operational are different claims.
