KRIM · Partner quality framework

Standards that build a dependable logistics network.

Clear expectations for partner eligibility, verified capabilities, safe service delivery, transparent commercial commitments and accountable shipment execution.

Important: These standards describe KRIM's intended partner expectations. They are not a signed contract, regulatory approval, licence, guarantee of assignments or substitute for applicable law. Only capabilities reviewed and explicitly activated for a defined scope may be represented as approved.

KRIM's partner network is capability-led. Partners remain responsible for the logistics services they provide, their personnel, licences, equipment, facilities, insurance and customer commitments. KRIM provides digital discovery and coordination workflows within the scope actually enabled and agreed.

1. Partner eligibility and legal identity

Applicants must provide accurate legal/business identity, constitution, operating address, authorised representative and tax information as applicable. The applicant must be lawfully entitled to perform the services it proposes to offer. Requirements differ by partner category, jurisdiction and the nature of the actual activity.

  • Valid identity and business registration evidence where applicable.
  • Authorised signatory and responsible operational contacts.
  • Tax registrations and bank account details relevant to the commercial arrangement.
  • Required licences, permits, registrations and professional qualifications.
  • Disclosure of material ownership, operating or compliance changes.

2. Verification and document integrity

KRIM may review submitted documents and verify information through appropriate sources. Applicants must provide complete, legible and current evidence and must not alter, fabricate, misrepresent or reuse another organisation's documents. Further checks may be required before or during participation.

Verification is limited to the checks actually completed. Uploading a document or receiving an application acknowledgement does not mean the document has been independently authenticated or that the partner is approved.

3. Service capabilities, locations and capacity

Partners must accurately declare the service modes, locations, routes, facilities, equipment, vehicles, staff, cargo classes, operating hours, lead times and capacity they can support. State exclusions and dependencies clearly. Do not claim nationwide, international, 24/7 or specialist coverage unless it is evidenced, current and authorised.

Any capability change that could affect accepted work must be reported promptly. Partner profiles and category listings are not proof of live capacity, service availability, route approval or a guaranteed assignment.

4. Service quality and customer commitments

  • Accept only work that matches verified capability and available resources.
  • Quote accurately and disclose all material inclusions, exclusions, assumptions and validity periods.
  • Meet the accepted pickup, delivery, handling and communication commitments.
  • Use competent personnel and suitable vehicles, equipment and facilities.
  • Maintain respectful, non-discriminatory and professional customer interactions.
  • Raise foreseeable service risks early and provide practical corrective options.

Numeric service levels, response times, performance thresholds, penalties or service credits apply only where separately agreed in writing. This framework does not create an unspecified service guarantee.

5. Safety, insurance and legal compliance

Partners must maintain the statutory licences, permits, registrations, insurance, trained personnel and safe operating conditions required for their actual services. Applicable requirements may include transport, motor vehicle, labour, workplace safety, tax, customs, environmental, consumer protection and data-protection laws.

Partners must disclose material safety incidents, licence suspensions, insurance lapses and regulatory investigations that could affect an accepted service. They must not accept prohibited or undeclared cargo, falsify documents, evade lawful inspections or offer improper payments.

Regulatory boundary: The legal classification of KRIM and each partner depends on actual activities and contracts. A technology-platform or partner-led description does not by itself remove statutory obligations. Obtain qualified legal advice on common-carrier registration and other applicable permissions before activating relevant operations.

6. Cargo handling, custody and documentation

Partners must follow agreed cargo-handling instructions, record handovers and discrepancies, protect cargo against foreseeable loss or damage and maintain an appropriate chain of custody. Cargo descriptions, quantities, weights, dimensions, declared values, seals and special-handling requirements must be accurate.

Required goods receipts, consignment notes, delivery records, e-way bills, customs documents and other records must be completed truthfully by the responsible party. Never mark a pickup or delivery complete before it occurs.

7. Commercial integrity and quotation standards

Quotes must be clear, current and linked to the specified service, route, cargo and validity period. Identify taxes, tolls, fuel adjustments, waiting, detention, loading, unloading, storage, demurrage, cancellation and other potential additional charges. Do not add undisclosed charges after acceptance except as allowed by the signed terms or mandatory law.

Rates, commissions, platform/referral fees, taxes, invoicing, deductions, payment timing and dispute procedures must be recorded in the applicable commercial schedule. KRIM does not promise minimum shipment volumes, guaranteed revenue, exclusivity or a standard commission through this page.

8. Shipment acceptance and execution

  1. Review the shipment details, route, cargo, schedule, service requirements and restrictions.
  2. Confirm actual capacity, required permissions, equipment and total cost.
  3. Submit a complete quote or bid and honour its stated validity.
  4. Wait for an authorised written assignment or booking confirmation.
  5. Confirm contacts, documents, pickup instructions, milestones and escalation route.
  6. Execute the accepted scope and obtain evidence at each required handover.

A request, quote submission, draft assignment or automated notification is not necessarily an accepted job. Partners must not start chargeable work without the required acceptance and approvals.

9. Shipment visibility and evidence quality

Provide timely and truthful status updates through the agreed platform or communication channel. Maintain accurate timestamps and supporting evidence for pickup, transit milestones, exceptions and delivery. Correct errors transparently; never fabricate, backdate or manipulate shipment events, proof of delivery or performance data.

10. Exceptions, complaints and claims

Immediately escalate material delays, failed pickups, accidents, cargo loss or damage, security concerns, regulatory holds and customer complaints through the agreed process. Protect people and cargo first, preserve relevant evidence and cooperate with investigations. Do not admit another party's liability, promise compensation or settle a claim without authority.

Liability, claims deadlines, insurance response and remedies are governed by the applicable law, shipment documents and signed agreement. This page does not create a liability cap or replace statutory rights.

11. Invoices, payment and financial records

Partners must issue accurate invoices for their own services and retain supporting shipment and tax records. Disputed charges, approved extras, credits, refunds and outstanding balances should be documented and reconciled through the agreed process.

Current proposed launch model: customers contract with and pay the logistics partner directly; the partner invoices the customer and records its own freight receipts. KRIM does not collect freight funds, hold escrow, split freight payments or execute partner payouts under this model. Any separately agreed KRIM referral, marketing or coordination fee must be documented and reviewed for applicable tax and legal treatment. This structure is not automatically GST-exempt.

12. Data protection and platform security

  • Use customer, driver, shipment and business information only for authorised and lawful purposes.
  • Give platform access only to named users who need it for their role.
  • Never share passwords, OTPs, API secrets or another user's credentials.
  • Protect devices, documents and exported records against unauthorised access.
  • Report suspected account compromise, data loss or privacy incidents promptly.
  • Retain or delete information according to contract and applicable legal requirements.

13. Professional and ethical conduct

Partners must act honestly, avoid conflicts of interest or disclose them, treat people fairly, protect cargo and customer property, and refrain from bribery, fraud, harassment, discrimination, retaliation or misleading claims. Do not use KRIM's name, logo, platform or customer relationships beyond the permissions granted in writing.

14. Performance monitoring and corrective action

Where contractually permitted, KRIM may review verified operational records, customer feedback, document validity, incident reports, claims, responsiveness and compliance status. A partner may be asked to provide clarification or a corrective action plan. New opportunities or platform access may be restricted while a material concern is investigated, in accordance with the applicable agreement and law.

Suspension or exit does not remove obligations for shipments already accepted, safe custody of cargo, customer continuity, records, confidentiality, claims or undisputed amounts due.

15. Partner onboarding and activation stages

Every status must describe the actual stage reached. Application is not approval; approval is not necessarily operational activation.

01 · Submitted

Initial details received; no service authority or assignment.

02 · Under review

Identity, evidence and proposed scope are assessed.

03 · Clarification

Missing or inconsistent information must be resolved.

04 · Scope approved

Specified capabilities and locations pass review.

05 · Activated

Agreement, permissions, commercial setup and required checks are complete.

06 · Reassessment

Participation may be paused for review of a material change or concern.

Activation checklist: legal identity and authority, capability evidence, applicable licences, insurance, signed agreement, defined commercial terms, authorised users, escalation contacts and a controlled test where appropriate.
