KRIM partner network

Reliable partners. Accountable execution.

KRIM’s network is built around verified capability, transparent shipment commitments and measurable service delivery. This framework explains the standards expected from prospective and active logistics partners.

KRIM seeks dependable transport, warehousing and related service providers whose actual capabilities can be verified. Network participation is selective and based on customer requirements, geography, service type, compliance, performance and commercial fit. KRIM does not publish partner counts, coverage claims or service guarantees unless supported by current verified records.

1. KRIM network model

KRIM operates a digital logistics coordination platform intended to connect customers with suitable logistics capabilities. Depending on the specific transaction, a partner may act as the contracting carrier, warehouse operator, customs or other specialist provider, subcontractor or another legally recognised service provider. The role, responsibilities and customer-facing disclosures must be defined in the relevant written agreement and shipment confirmation.

Partner status does not automatically make a provider an agent of KRIM or authorise it to bind KRIM. Neither party should make commitments, collect money, issue documents or use the other party’s name beyond the authority expressly granted in writing. Legal duties arising from actual conduct remain applicable regardless of contract labels.

2. Partner eligibility

Prospective partners should be legally established or otherwise lawfully entitled to provide the proposed service, with appropriate identity, tax, banking, operating and regulatory details. Depending on the service, onboarding may require:

  • Business identity, constitution, address, PAN and GST details where applicable.
  • Bank account proof and authorised signatory details for commercial settlement.
  • Transport, carrier, warehouse, customs, safety or other licences and registrations required for the activity.
  • Vehicle, driver, equipment, facility and insurance details relevant to the proposed capability.
  • Service locations, operating hours, capacity, supported cargo types and route limitations.
  • References, past performance information and declarations of conflicts or material incidents where reasonably requested.

Requirements vary by partner type and jurisdiction. KRIM may decline or pause onboarding where required information is missing, inconsistent or cannot be verified.

3. Identity, document and capability verification

KRIM may check submitted records against issuing authorities, registries, insurers, references or other reliable sources, subject to applicable law and consent requirements. Verification is proportionate to the proposed service and may be refreshed periodically or after a material change.

Partners must promptly disclose expired, suspended, cancelled or changed licences, insurance, ownership, banking details, operating address, key personnel or service capacity. A document upload or onboarding status is not a guarantee that every statement has been independently audited. KRIM may request original or certified evidence before activation.

4. Capability, territory and availability

Partners must describe actual and current operating coverage, modes, vehicle or facility types, capacity, cargo restrictions, service hours, lead times and any subcontracting arrangements. Do not claim a route, location, fleet, warehouse or service capability that is not available and authorised.

India-first partner acquisition is the current commercial focus. International onboarding may occur only after route-specific legal, customs, insurance, payment and operational checks. A partner profile, opportunity listing or quote response is not a guarantee of an assignment or active coverage.

5. Service and customer-experience standards

  • Accept only work that matches verified capability, legal permissions and available capacity.
  • Provide accurate rates, inclusions, exclusions, transit estimates and quote validity.
  • Meet agreed pickup, handling, transit, delivery and communication commitments or raise risks promptly.
  • Use trained personnel and suitable vehicles, equipment, facilities and protective handling procedures.
  • Keep shipment records, handover notes, scans, proof of delivery and incident evidence accurate.
  • Protect cargo and customer information and avoid unauthorised substitutions or diversions.
  • Escalate safety, regulatory, security, delay, loss, damage and service failures without concealment.

Any numeric SLA, service credit, performance target or penalty applies only if expressly agreed in a signed partner schedule or shipment contract. This framework does not create an unpriced or unmeasured service guarantee.

6. Safety, insurance and regulatory compliance

Partners are responsible for maintaining the licences, registrations, permits, trained staff, safe equipment, statutory records and insurance required for the services they actually perform. They must comply with applicable transport, labour, tax, road safety, environmental, customs, data protection and other laws in each relevant jurisdiction.

Partners must disclose cargo exclusions and special-handling limitations before quoting. They must not accept prohibited or undeclared cargo, falsify records, facilitate bribery, evade lawful inspections or use unapproved subcontractors. Insurance requirements, insured limits, exclusions and evidence must be stated in the signed agreement and verified for the relevant operation.

Regulatory reminder: A partner-led or marketplace model does not automatically remove statutory duties from KRIM or a partner. Whether a party requires common-carrier registration, other transport authorisation, e-commerce eligibility or a different legal structure depends on the actual activity and applicable law. Obtain written professional advice before activating regulated services.

7. Rates, quotations and commercial terms

Partners must submit rates that are accurate, current, route-specific and clear about included services, taxes, tolls, waiting, loading, unloading, detention, storage, fuel adjustments, return movements and other potential charges. Quote validity and assumptions must be explicit. No undisclosed surcharge may be added after acceptance except where authorised by the agreed contract or mandatory law.

Commission, platform fees, taxes, deductions, currency, invoicing, payment milestones, settlement timing, dispute windows and any service credits must be set out in a separately accepted commercial schedule. This public framework does not promise a particular commission percentage, assignment volume, minimum revenue or payment date.

8. Shipment opportunity and acceptance

  1. Review the complete shipment description, origin, destination, cargo, schedule and required service.
  2. Check capacity, route permissions, vehicle or facility suitability and all relevant costs.
  3. Submit a complete quote or bid within the stated validity period.
  4. Wait for the authorised customer/KRIM workflow to accept the offer and issue a written assignment or booking confirmation.
  5. Confirm operational contact, pickup instructions, documents, milestones and escalation path before dispatch.

A bid, draft allocation, notification or verbal discussion is not an accepted assignment. Partners must not collect customer funds or represent that a shipment is booked unless the contract and transaction workflow expressly authorise it.

9. Shipment visibility, records and evidence

Partners must provide timely, truthful status events through the agreed channel. Events should be recorded at the point they occur where practicable and must not be fabricated, backdated or marked complete before the underlying event has happened.

Where agreed, partners must provide pickup proof, package counts, exception notes, transit milestones, delivery confirmation, recipient details and supporting documents. Access to customer or shipment data must be limited to personnel who need it to perform the service and retained only as required by contract and law.

10. Delays, incidents, loss and claims

Partners must promptly notify KRIM of material delays, failed pickups, delivery attempts, accidents, cargo loss or damage, security incidents, regulatory holds, complaints and circumstances likely to affect an agreed service. Preserve cargo, packaging, photographs, vehicle/facility records, communications and other evidence. Do not admit liability, promise compensation, dispose of cargo or settle a claim on behalf of another party unless authorised.

Responsibility, liability limits, notice periods, insurance claims and customer remedies must be governed by the applicable signed agreement, shipment documents, insurance policy and mandatory law. This page does not cap liability or replace any statutory obligation.

11. Invoices, reconciliation and settlement

Partners must submit accurate invoices and supporting proof for completed services in the format and timeframe set out in the commercial agreement. The invoice should identify the correct legal supplier, tax details, shipment references, agreed rate, approved extras, applicable taxes and any authorised adjustments.

KRIM will reconcile amounts according to the agreed workflow and contract. A payment status such as initiated, pending, failed, reversed or settled must not be misrepresented. Disputed amounts should be documented and handled through the agreed reconciliation process. No settlement timing or amount is promised by this general framework.

12. Professional conduct, confidentiality and data

  • Treat customers, drivers, staff, other partners and public authorities professionally and without discrimination or harassment.
  • Do not offer or accept bribes, falsify compliance records, manipulate tracking events or misappropriate cargo or funds.
  • Keep rates, customer information, credentials, operational data and confidential business information secure.
  • Use KRIM names, marks, platform access and customer communications only as authorised.
  • Report suspected fraud, cyber incidents, privacy breaches, safety hazards and conflicts of interest promptly.
  • Do not transfer customer data to unrelated parties or use it for independent marketing without a lawful basis and required permissions.

13. Performance review, suspension and exit

KRIM may review partner performance using verified service records, customer feedback, document validity, incident history, responsiveness, claims and compliance status. Where appropriate, KRIM may request corrective action, restrict new assignments, pause access or terminate participation in accordance with the signed agreement and applicable law.

Immediate protective action may be necessary where there is a credible safety, fraud, security, legal or cargo-integrity concern. Suspension does not remove obligations relating to shipments already accepted, customer property, records, confidentiality, outstanding claims or lawful payment of undisputed amounts.

14. Onboarding and activation stages

Partner onboarding is a controlled review, not automatic approval. The recorded status should reflect the actual review:

Submitted

Initial enquiry received; no service authority or assignment is created.

Under review

Identity, documents, proposed service scope and capability are being assessed.

Clarification required

Additional or corrected information is needed; this is not approval.

Approved for defined scope

Specified services and locations pass review; approval does not guarantee work or activate every service.

Operationally activated

Required agreement, legal and insurance checks, commercial setup, access controls and applicable launch tests are complete for the defined scope.

Paused / reassessment

New assignments may be restricted while a document, capability, safety, compliance or performance concern is reviewed.

Only explicitly activated services may be offered through KRIM. Material changes to ownership, licences, insurance, territory, fleet, facilities or subcontractors may require reassessment.

15. Apply to the KRIM network

Interested transporters, fleet operators, warehouse providers and other logistics specialists can first create a partner account, then submit a partner/network enquiry through KRIM’s Contact workflow. Account creation establishes a login identity only; it does not complete business verification, approve a partner, activate a capability or create an assignment. Provide your business identity, service category, operating locations and a reliable contact method. Do not send passwords, OTPs or unnecessary sensitive personal data.

KRIM may request further documents after an initial review. Submission does not guarantee approval, listing, exclusivity, work allocation or revenue. A partner may begin performing services only after verification is completed, the required agreement is signed, any regulatory preconditions are satisfied and a specific assignment is accepted.

Before activation: KRIM must complete partner due diligence, role and legal classification, licence checks, insurance review, service capability validation, commercial agreement, payment and tax setup, escalation mapping and a controlled test shipment where appropriate.
