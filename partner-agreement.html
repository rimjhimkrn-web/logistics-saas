KRIM partner documentation

A clear agreement for reliable execution.

A structured framework for documenting the relationship between KRIM Logistics and an independent transport, warehousing or specialist service provider—before any shipment is accepted.

India-first partner networkShipment-specific schedulesUpdated 4 November 2026
Legal and activation notice: This is a discussion and drafting framework only. It is not an executed agreement, offer of work, carrier appointment, guarantee of volume or authority to operate. It must be adapted and reviewed by an Indian logistics lawyer, completed with the correct legal entity details and signed by authorised parties before use. Do not use it to accept paid shipments until KRIM's own legal eligibility, registrations and regulatory position have been confirmed in writing.

Execution checklist

This framework is intended to help the parties negotiate a written agreement. It deliberately leaves commercial variables and legal choices to be completed in the signed contract and its schedules. The actual relationship is determined by the parties' conduct as well as the written terms; labels such as “platform”, “independent partner” or “intermediary” do not override applicable law.

1. Parties, legal identity and relationship

The final agreement must identify the full legal name, constitution, registered/principal address, tax details and authorised signatory of each party. “KRIM Logistics” must be replaced or supplemented with the exact legal name of the proprietor/entity that is lawfully entering the agreement. The partner's full legal identity and service-provider capacity must also be recorded.

The agreement must state whether the partner contracts directly with the customer, with KRIM, or through another disclosed structure for each service. It must not inaccurately describe an employment, agency, partnership, franchise or carrier relationship. Nothing in this draft creates authority for either party to bind the other.

2. Purpose, services and territory

The signed scope schedule must identify the services actually authorised: for example road transport, rail/air/ocean forwarding, warehousing, handling or a defined specialist service. It must state the approved lanes/locations, cargo classes, equipment, capacity, operating hours, exclusions and any required permits. No service or territory is active merely because it appears on KRIM's website or in a partner profile.

Each party must state its role in customer contracting, service execution, custody of goods, issuance of transport documents, customer communication, collection of funds and claims handling. Where different roles apply to different shipments, the relevant work order must make the allocation clear.

3. Conditions before activation

Partner onboarding follows the review stages described in the Partner Standards: Submitted, Under review, Clarification required, Approved for defined scope, Operationally activated, and Paused / reassessment. Account creation or document submission alone does not confer approval. A partner may be activated only for the specific services and locations that have passed review.

No partner may be marked active for a service until the following have been completed and checked as applicable:

  • Identity, constitution, address, tax and authorised-signatory verification.
  • Current licences, registrations, permits and professional qualifications required for the proposed service.
  • Vehicle, driver, facility, equipment and insurance evidence appropriate to the service.
  • Bank-account verification and agreed invoice/tax-document process.
  • Signed agreement, completed schedules and accepted operating procedures.
  • KRIM's own legal eligibility, regulatory registrations and payment/customer-disclosure controls for the proposed transaction model.
Non-waivable gate: The Carriage by Road Act, 2007 defines “common carrier” broadly and expressly includes certain goods-booking companies, contractors, agents and brokers. Section 3 prohibits engaging in that business without the required certificate of registration. The parties must obtain advice on whether KRIM's actual activities fall within the definition before road-freight operations are activated. A partner-led structure alone is not a safe harbour.

4. Shipment work orders and acceptance

Every shipment must be governed by a written or electronically authenticated work order/booking confirmation that identifies at least: customer and contracting parties; shipment reference; origin and destination; cargo description, quantity and declared value; pickup and delivery windows; service scope; agreed price and taxes; accessorial charges; documents; insurance arrangements; cargo restrictions; liability terms; cancellation rules; milestones; and escalation contacts.

A request, bid, quote, system notification or draft allocation is not an accepted assignment. The partner must expressly accept the final work order and must not dispatch, collect cargo or incur chargeable costs until the authorised acceptance and required documents are available. Any change must be recorded and approved by the authorised parties.

5. Partner duties and service standards

  • Perform only the services, routes and cargo categories for which the partner is qualified, authorised and insured.
  • Provide accurate availability, capacity, rates, transit estimates, exclusions and quote validity.
  • Use properly licensed drivers, trained staff, roadworthy vehicles and suitable handling equipment/facilities.
  • Protect cargo, maintain chain-of-custody records and follow agreed handling instructions.
  • Provide truthful pickup, in-transit, exception and delivery updates through the agreed channel.
  • Notify the relevant parties immediately of delay, accident, loss, damage, shortage, seizure, safety event or suspected fraud.
  • Maintain records and cooperate with reasonable audits, investigations and lawful authority requests.

Specific service levels, reporting intervals, credits or performance remedies must be set out in an agreed schedule. No unpriced or undefined service level is created by this framework.

6. KRIM responsibilities and limits of authority

The signed agreement must state precisely what KRIM will provide, such as platform access, lead or opportunity routing, workflow records, agreed coordination, support or payment administration. It must not imply that KRIM provides transport, holds cargo, insures goods, collects funds or guarantees delivery unless that is the actual and legally authorised role for the relevant transaction.

KRIM must provide the partner with the shipment information and customer instructions it has agreed to supply, identify the authorised communication and escalation channels, and communicate any platform or workflow changes that materially affect performance. Each party remains responsible for its own personnel, acts, omissions and legal duties.

7. Cargo custody, declarations and transport documents

The parties must agree who checks packaging, quantity, seals, labels, weight, dimensions, cargo condition and declared value at each handover. Discrepancies must be recorded before acceptance wherever practicable. The consignor must provide accurate cargo descriptions and disclose dangerous, hazardous, restricted, temperature-sensitive, high-value or otherwise special cargo before quotation and acceptance.

For road carriage, the parties must use the goods forwarding note, goods receipt and other records required by applicable law. The agreement must allocate responsibility for preparing, checking, issuing, signing and retaining each document. No party may falsify a declaration, receipt, proof of delivery, e-way bill or customs record.

8. Rates, invoicing, taxes and settlement

The commercial schedule must specify the currency, base rate, route/service basis, taxes, tolls, fuel adjustments, loading/unloading, waiting, detention, storage, demurrage, return movement, cancellation and any other accessorial charge. It must identify which charges are included, excluded or subject to prior approval. No undisclosed surcharge may be added after acceptance except where authorised by the contract or mandatory law.

It must also state the invoice issuer, invoice recipient, GST treatment, supporting documents, invoice submission deadline, dispute window, payment milestones, settlement method, deductions, platform/coordination fee if any, and the responsible party for payment. No minimum shipment volume, exclusivity, commission percentage or payment date is promised unless expressly written in the signed schedule.

Where KRIM or a partner collects customer funds, the agreement and customer-facing checkout must consistently identify the collector, supplier, invoice issuer, refund decision-maker and payment-settlement obligations. Payment gateway status alone does not determine contractual entitlement.

9. Liability, loss, damage, delay and claims

The final agreement must allocate responsibility according to the actual contracting and performing roles, custody periods, applicable statutory regime and evidence. It must define claim notification channels, supporting records, investigation cooperation, mitigation, response milestones and any agreed recourse between the parties. It must not use a blanket exclusion or cap that conflicts with mandatory law.

For road carriage governed by the Carriage by Road Act, 2007, the Act contains specific provisions on common-carrier responsibility, goods documentation, liability and restrictions on contractual limitations. The parties must have counsel draft this section against the current Act, Rules, facts and any other applicable transport law. Do not copy generic foreign carrier terms or insert an arbitrary per-kilogram cap.

Each party should remain responsible for its own fraud, wilful misconduct, negligence, breach of confidentiality, unlawful conduct and statutory obligations to the extent provided by law and the negotiated contract. Any indemnity, exclusion, consequential-loss provision or liability cap must be specifically reviewed and legally enforceable.

10. Insurance and risk management

The schedule must identify the insurance policies required for each service, including relevant motor, public liability, employer/workmen, warehouse, carrier liability or cargo cover as appropriate. Record insurer, policy number, insured party, limit, deductible, territory, covered activity, exclusions and expiry date. Obtain certificates and, where necessary, policy wording or endorsements.

Insurance must not be described as cargo protection unless the actual policy covers the cargo, party, route, value and event in question. The parties must agree who arranges optional cargo insurance, who pays the premium, who is named as insured/beneficiary and who submits claims. Insurance does not replace statutory liability.

11. Legal, regulatory and ethical compliance

Each party must comply with laws applicable to its own role and performance, including transport and motor vehicle rules, common-carrier registration where applicable, GST and tax, labour and workplace safety, environmental requirements, customs and trade controls, sanctions, anti-bribery rules, consumer protection and data protection. Required registrations must be maintained throughout the agreement.

Neither party may carry prohibited or undeclared goods, make improper payments, evade inspections, falsify records, misuse permits or represent that it holds an approval it does not hold. A party must notify the other promptly of an investigation, suspension, cancellation or material compliance breach that could affect a shipment or this agreement.

12. Confidentiality, customer data and platform access

Each party must use confidential information and personal data only for the agreed service and lawful purposes, restrict access to personnel who need it, apply reasonable security controls, and prevent unauthorised disclosure, copying or sale. The agreement must specify the parties' data-protection roles, lawful basis, permitted processing, retention/deletion, security incident notice, data-subject request support and cross-border transfer arrangements as applicable.

Partner credentials must be individual and must not be shared. The partner must promptly report suspected account compromise, data loss or unauthorised access. On termination, access must be disabled and data returned or securely deleted subject to lawful retention obligations.

13. Subcontracting and assignment

The partner may not subcontract, reassign, transfer custody or substitute a carrier, vehicle, warehouse or other service provider without prior written approval where required by the agreement or law. Any approved subcontractor must meet equivalent qualification, insurance, safety, confidentiality and compliance standards. The primary partner remains responsible to the extent stated in the contract and applicable law.

Neither party may assign this agreement or transfer a material obligation without the other party's written consent, except for any permitted corporate reorganisation or transfer expressly agreed in the signed contract.

14. Disruptions, emergencies and force majeure

The agreement must define qualifying events beyond a party's reasonable control, notice requirements, mitigation, evidence, continuity measures and the effect on affected work orders. The affected party must notify the other promptly, protect cargo and people, provide realistic revised estimates and obtain instructions before incurring avoidable additional costs.

Force majeure must not excuse payment for completed services, fraud, deliberate misconduct, failure to maintain required licences/insurance or obligations that cannot lawfully be suspended. If a shipment is already in transit or storage when an event occurs, the parties must agree safe custody, rerouting, return, storage and customer communications.

15. Term, suspension and termination

The signed agreement must state its start date, initial term, renewal method and termination notice period. It should permit immediate suspension or termination for material safety risk, loss of required authorisation or insurance, fraud, serious data breach, prohibited cargo, insolvency or material breach not remedied within an agreed cure period, subject to mandatory law.

On expiry or termination, the parties must stop accepting new work, settle undisputed amounts, return property and credentials, protect records, and cooperate on open claims. They must agree in advance how cargo in transit, stored goods, customer communications, refunds and outstanding work orders will be completed or transferred safely. Clauses intended to survive—including confidentiality, data, payment, liability and dispute provisions—must be identified.

16. Disputes, notices and governing law

The final agreement must state the governing law, courts with jurisdiction and, if selected, a properly drafted arbitration clause identifying the seat, venue, language, appointment method and applicable arbitration rules. It must not impose an unfair or unlawful restriction on consumer remedies or statutory forums.

Formal notice addresses, authorised email addresses, delivery methods and deemed-receipt rules must be completed for both parties. Operational escalation contacts should be listed separately and must not replace formal legal notices unless the contract expressly says so.

17. Mandatory schedules to complete before signature

Schedule A — Parties & authority

Exact legal names, constitution, addresses, tax IDs, signatories and notice details.

Schedule B — Service scope

Modes, routes, locations, capacity, cargo categories, equipment, exclusions and subcontractors.

Schedule C — Commercials

Rates, taxes, platform fee if any, invoicing, deductions, payment timing and dispute process.

Schedule D — Operating SLA

Pickup/delivery windows, milestones, status frequency, evidence, escalation and remedies.

Schedule E — Insurance & licences

Policy limits, exclusions, expiry, permits, registrations and renewal evidence.

Schedule F — Shipment work order

Shipment-level parties, cargo, value, route, price, documents, risk, cancellation and acceptance.

Attach the applicable privacy/data-processing terms, safety procedures, prohibited cargo list, claims protocol and any country-specific annex where required.

18. Execution checklist and legal review

  1. Confirm the legal entity that will contract as KRIM and its authority to enter this arrangement.
  2. Obtain written advice on e-commerce entity eligibility, common-carrier registration and any other licence/registration triggered by the actual business model.
  3. Confirm the partner's legal status, service permissions, insurance and bank account independently.
  4. Complete all schedules and reconcile this agreement with the customer Terms & Conditions, Privacy Notice, Refund & Cancellation Policy, Shipping & Delivery Policy and actual checkout disclosures.
  5. Confirm tax invoices, collection and settlement flows, claims handling and the actual Razorpay/payment configuration.
  6. Have both parties sign the final version; store the executed copy and evidence of authority securely.
  7. Run a controlled, legally cleared test shipment before opening the service to customers.
Current KRIM status disclosure: This public page does not mean KRIM has approved partners, executed this agreement with any provider, activated every transport mode or obtained every required registration. Only a separately verified and signed partner record plus a shipment-specific accepted work order can evidence a live relationship.
