Partner Agreement Framework

A structured starting point for documenting the relationship between KRIM Logistics and independent transport, warehousing, forwarding and specialist service providers.

Important legal notice: This page is an informational drafting framework, not a signed contract, legal opinion, offer of work, appointment or guarantee of shipment volume. It must be adapted to the actual parties and transaction model, reviewed by a qualified Indian lawyer and signed by authorised representatives before it is relied on. Do not begin regulated or paid operations until all required legal, tax and regulatory approvals are confirmed.

Use this framework to identify the topics that the final agreement must address. It deliberately leaves legal choices, commercial variables and shipment-specific obligations to be completed in the signed agreement and its schedules. The parties' actual conduct matters; labels such as “platform”, “independent partner” or “intermediary” do not override applicable law.

1. Parties, legal identity and relationship

The final agreement must identify each party's exact legal name, constitution, principal address, tax details and authorised signatory. “KRIM Logistics” must be supplemented by the exact legal name of the proprietor or entity entering the contract. The partner's legal identity and capacity to provide the relevant service must also be recorded.

For each service, clearly state who contracts with the customer, who performs the service, who holds cargo, who issues transport documents, who collects payment and who handles claims. Do not inaccurately describe an employment, agency, partnership, franchise or carrier relationship. Neither party may bind the other unless expressly authorised in writing.

2. Services, scope and territory

The signed scope schedule must list only services the partner is qualified and authorised to perform: for example road transport, rail/air/ocean forwarding, warehousing, handling or a defined specialist service. Specify approved routes and locations, cargo classes, equipment, capacity, operating hours, exclusions and permits. A website category or partner profile does not itself establish live coverage, capacity or approval.

For each service, allocate responsibility for customer contracting, physical execution, cargo custody, transport documents, customer communication, collections and claims. If roles vary by shipment, the work order must identify the applicable arrangement.

3. Conditions before partner activation

Onboarding should distinguish application, review, clarification, approval for a defined scope and operational activation. Submitting documents or creating an account does not confer approval. A partner may participate only in the specific services and locations that have passed the applicable review.

  • Identity, legal constitution, address, tax and authorised-signatory checks.
  • Current licences, registrations, permits and qualifications required for the service.
  • Vehicle, driver, facility, equipment, safety and insurance evidence as applicable.
  • Bank-account verification and an agreed invoice/tax-document process.
  • Signed agreement, completed schedules and accepted operating procedures.
  • Confirmation that KRIM's own business model and activities are legally permitted and any required registrations are in place.
Road-freight legal review is essential. India's Carriage by Road Act, 2007 defines “common carrier” to include certain goods-booking companies, contractors, agents and brokers. Sections 3 and 4 address registration requirements for persons engaging in that business. Whether KRIM's actual activities fall within the definition depends on the facts and must be assessed by qualified Indian counsel before relevant operations begin. A partner-led structure alone does not settle that question. Read the Act on India Code.

4. Shipment-specific work orders and acceptance

Each shipment should have a written or electronically authenticated work order/booking confirmation identifying the customer and contracting parties; shipment reference; origin and destination; cargo description, quantity and declared value; pickup/delivery windows; agreed service; price and taxes; additional charges; required documents; insurance arrangements; cargo restrictions; cancellation terms; milestones and escalation contacts.

A request, bid, quote, draft allocation or system notification is not necessarily an accepted assignment. The partner should expressly accept the final work order and must not dispatch, collect cargo or incur chargeable costs until the required approvals and documents are available. Changes must be recorded and approved by the authorised parties.

5. Partner responsibilities and service standards

  • Perform only services, routes and cargo categories for which the partner is qualified, authorised and insured.
  • Provide accurate availability, capacity, rates, transit estimates, exclusions and quote validity.
  • Use appropriately licensed personnel, roadworthy vehicles and suitable handling equipment or facilities.
  • Protect cargo, maintain chain-of-custody records and follow agreed handling instructions.
  • Provide truthful pickup, in-transit, exception and delivery updates through agreed channels.
  • Promptly report delays, accidents, loss, damage, shortage, seizure, safety events or suspected fraud.
  • Maintain records and cooperate with reasonable audits, investigations and lawful authority requests.

Specific service levels, reporting intervals, credits and performance remedies must be stated in an agreed schedule. This framework does not create undefined or unpriced service levels.

6. KRIM responsibilities and limits

The final agreement must state exactly what KRIM provides, such as platform access, opportunity routing, workflow records, agreed coordination or support. KRIM must not imply it provides transport, holds cargo, insures goods or guarantees delivery unless that is its actual and legally authorised role in the relevant transaction.

Current proposed launch model: the customer contracts with and pays the logistics provider directly. KRIM does not collect freight charges, hold escrow, split freight funds or execute partner payouts under this model. Any separately agreed KRIM referral, marketing or coordination fee must be documented and reviewed for applicable legal and tax treatment. A future funds-collection or settlement model requires separate legal, tax and regulatory review before implementation or public representation.

KRIM should provide the information and coordination expressly agreed with the partner, identify authorised communication channels and communicate material workflow changes. Each party remains responsible for its own personnel, acts, omissions and statutory obligations.

7. Cargo custody, declarations and documents

Allocate who checks packaging, quantity, seals, labels, weight, dimensions, cargo condition and declared value at each handover. Record discrepancies before acceptance wherever practicable. The consignor must accurately describe cargo and disclose dangerous, hazardous, restricted, temperature-sensitive, high-value or special cargo before quotation and acceptance.

For road carriage, use the goods forwarding note, goods receipt and other records required by applicable law. Allocate responsibility for preparing, checking, issuing, signing and retaining each document. No party may falsify a declaration, receipt, proof of delivery, e-way bill or customs record.

8. Rates, invoices, taxes and payment

The commercial schedule must state currency, base rate, route/service basis, taxes, tolls, fuel adjustments, loading/unloading, waiting, detention, storage, demurrage, return movement, cancellation and other accessorial charges. Specify what is included, excluded or subject to prior approval. No undisclosed surcharge should be added after acceptance except as permitted by the contract or mandatory law.

Also specify invoice issuer and recipient, GST treatment, supporting documents, invoice deadline, dispute window, payment milestones, settlement method, deductions, any platform/coordination fee and the responsible payer. Do not imply a minimum shipment volume, exclusivity, commission percentage or payment date unless it is expressly included in the signed schedule.

Under the proposed launch model, the logistics provider invoices the customer and receives freight payment directly. KRIM may invoice a separately agreed referral, marketing or coordination fee only under a documented arrangement and after applicable legal and tax review. No escrow, split settlement, freight collection or partner payout is offered under this model.

9. Liability, loss, damage, delay and claims

The signed agreement must allocate responsibility according to the actual contracting and performing roles, custody periods, applicable statutory regime and evidence. Define claim-notification channels, supporting records, investigation cooperation, mitigation, response milestones and any agreed recourse. Do not use a blanket exclusion or cap that conflicts with mandatory law.

For road carriage governed by the Carriage by Road Act, 2007, the Act addresses common-carrier responsibility, goods documentation and liability. Have counsel draft this section against the current Act, Rules and actual transaction structure. Do not copy generic foreign carrier terms or insert an arbitrary per-kilogram cap.

Each party should remain responsible for its own fraud, wilful misconduct, negligence, breach of confidentiality, unlawful conduct and statutory obligations to the extent provided by applicable law and the negotiated contract. Indemnities, exclusions, consequential-loss provisions and liability caps require specific legal review.

10. Insurance and risk management

Specify the insurance required for each service, which may include motor, public liability, employer/workmen, warehouse, carrier liability or cargo cover as appropriate. Record insurer, policy number, insured party, limit, deductible, territory, covered activity, exclusions and expiry. Obtain certificates and policy wording or endorsements where needed.

Do not describe insurance as cargo protection unless the actual policy covers the cargo, party, route, value and event. Agree who arranges optional cargo insurance, who pays the premium, who is insured/beneficiary and who submits claims. Insurance does not replace statutory liability.

11. Legal, regulatory and ethical compliance

Each party must comply with laws applicable to its role and performance, including transport and motor-vehicle rules, common-carrier registration where applicable, GST and tax, labour and workplace safety, environmental requirements, customs and trade controls, sanctions, anti-bribery rules, consumer protection and data protection. Required registrations and permits must be maintained.

Neither party may carry prohibited or undeclared goods, make improper payments, evade inspections, falsify records, misuse permits or claim an approval it does not hold. Notify the other party promptly of an investigation, suspension, cancellation or material compliance breach that could affect a shipment or this agreement.

12. Confidentiality, personal data and platform access

Use confidential information and personal data only for agreed and lawful purposes. Restrict access to personnel who need it, apply reasonable security controls and prevent unauthorised disclosure, copying or sale. The final contract should specify the parties' data-protection roles, lawful basis, permitted processing, retention/deletion, incident notification, data-subject request support and cross-border transfer arrangements as applicable.

Partner credentials must be individual and must not be shared. Report suspected account compromise, data loss or unauthorised access promptly. On termination, disable access and return or securely delete data subject to lawful retention obligations.

13. Subcontracting and assignment

Do not subcontract, reassign, transfer custody or substitute a carrier, vehicle, warehouse or service provider without prior written approval where required by the agreement or law. Approved subcontractors must meet equivalent qualification, insurance, safety, confidentiality and compliance standards. The primary partner remains responsible to the extent stated in the contract and applicable law.

Neither party may assign this agreement or transfer a material obligation without the other's written consent, except for any expressly permitted transfer set out in the signed contract.

14. Disruption, emergencies and force majeure

Define qualifying events beyond a party's reasonable control, notice requirements, mitigation, evidence, continuity measures and effects on affected work orders. The affected party must notify the other promptly, protect cargo and people, provide realistic revised estimates and obtain instructions before incurring avoidable additional costs.

Force majeure should not excuse payment for completed services, fraud, deliberate misconduct, failure to maintain required licences or insurance, or obligations that cannot lawfully be suspended. For cargo already in transit or storage, agree safe custody, rerouting, return, storage and customer communications.

15. Term, suspension and termination

State the effective date, initial term, renewal method and termination notice period. Provide for suspension or termination in appropriate cases such as material safety risk, loss of required authorisation or insurance, fraud, serious data breach, prohibited cargo, insolvency or material breach not remedied within an agreed cure period, subject to mandatory law.

On expiry or termination, stop accepting new work, settle undisputed amounts, return property and credentials, protect records and cooperate on open claims. Agree how cargo in transit or storage, customer communications, refunds and outstanding work orders will be completed or transferred safely. Identify clauses intended to survive, including confidentiality, data, payment, liability and dispute provisions.

16. Disputes, notices and governing law

State governing law, courts with jurisdiction and, if selected, a properly drafted arbitration clause identifying the seat, venue, language, appointment method and applicable rules. Do not impose an unfair or unlawful restriction on consumer remedies or statutory forums.

Complete formal notice addresses, authorised email addresses, delivery methods and deemed-receipt rules for both parties. Operational escalation contacts should be listed separately and should not replace formal legal notices unless the signed contract expressly provides otherwise.

17. Schedules to complete before signature

Schedule A — Parties & authority

Exact legal names, constitution, addresses, tax IDs, signatories and notice details.

Schedule B — Service scope

Modes, routes, locations, capacity, cargo categories, equipment, exclusions and subcontractors.

Schedule C — Commercial terms

Rates, taxes, any platform fee, invoicing, deductions, payment timing and dispute process.

Schedule D — Operating standards

Pickup/delivery windows, milestones, status frequency, evidence, escalation and remedies.

Schedule E — Insurance & licences

Policy limits, exclusions, expiry, permits, registrations and renewal evidence.

Schedule F — Shipment work order

Shipment-level parties, cargo, value, route, price, documents, risk, cancellation and acceptance.

Attach applicable privacy/data-processing terms, safety procedures, prohibited-cargo rules, claims protocol and country-specific annexes where required.

18. Execution checklist and legal review

  1. Confirm the exact legal entity that will contract as KRIM and its authority to enter the arrangement.
  2. Obtain written advice on whether KRIM's actual activities trigger common-carrier registration or other licence/registration requirements.
  3. Verify the partner's legal status, service permissions, insurance and bank account independently.
  4. Complete all schedules and reconcile this agreement with customer terms, privacy, refund/cancellation and delivery policies.
  5. Confirm tax invoices, direct customer-to-partner payment flow, any separate KRIM fee, claims handling and actual payment configuration.
  6. Have both parties sign the final version; store the executed copy and evidence of signatory authority securely.
  7. Run a controlled test shipment only after legal and operational clearance.
Status disclosure: This public framework does not mean KRIM has executed an agreement with any provider, approved a partner, activated every transport mode or obtained every required registration. A live relationship requires a separately verified partner record, a signed agreement and an accepted shipment-specific work order where applicable.
