Cross-dock operating flow

Every inbound unit must retain its shipment, leg, quantity, quality and custody identity through sortation and outbound handover.

01
Inbound identification

Match arrival, receipt evidence, shipment leg, carrier, destination and expected onward movement.

02
Condition & quantity check

Verify labels, piece count, damage, seals, temperature requirements and quality restrictions before flow-through.

03
Sortation & staging

Route to destination lane, staging zone or consolidation wave using compatible freight and handling rules.

04
Outbound confirmation

Reconcile all included units against the outbound shipment leg and complete authorised handover.

Consolidation compatibility

A group is valid only when all shipment and handling constraints are satisfied.

⌖
Destination & route
Compatible delivery market, route plan, service level and outbound cut-off.
▣
Handling & equipment
Dimensions, weight, stackability, pallet/container compatibility and loading sequence.
⚑
Compliance & special cargo
Dangerous goods, temperature, customs, segregation and document requirements.
✓
Capacity & service promise
Verified carrier capacity, dock slot, transit plan and customer delivery commitments.

Custody & exception controls

Consolidation must never erase the identity or status of the original freight.

↔
Parent-child traceability

Retain every source shipment, leg, handling unit, item, lot and quantity within the consolidated movement.

◎
Quantity reconciliation

Compare inbound received, sorted, grouped, held and outbound loaded quantities; block unexplained differences.

⚠
Exception segregation

Isolate damaged, unidentified, non-compliant or destination-mismatched freight from dispatchable groups.

⌁
Event-level custody

Capture scan, actor, facility, location, event time and evidence for each handover or re-grouping.
