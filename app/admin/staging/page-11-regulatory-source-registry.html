Regulatory Source Registry

Maintain a governed catalogue of authoritative regulatory sources that inform country, corridor, transport-mode and service compliance. Track provenance, jurisdiction, applicability, freshness, interpretation ownership and the operational controls affected by each source.

Regulatory intelligence · Source governance

Make every compliance decision traceable to a credible, current source.

KRIM operates across jurisdictions with different legal systems, regulators, languages and update cycles. This registry establishes how official sources are identified, assessed, reviewed and linked to readiness and activation controls. It is not a substitute for qualified local legal or regulatory advice.

01 · Source identity

Authority and provenance

Capture the issuing authority and original publication location so reviewers can distinguish primary legal material from commentary or third-party summaries.

  • Regulator, ministry, court or official body
  • Official source title and canonical reference
  • Publication and effective dates
  • Original language and translation status
02 · Applicability

Jurisdiction and service scope

Map each source to the places and activities it governs. Avoid applying a national rule to a local permit or a mode-specific requirement outside its scope.

  • Country, state/province and local authority
  • Mode, service and regulated activity
  • Entity, partner and professional role
  • Corridor and cross-border applicability
03 · Review governance

Evidence and interpretation control

Record who reviewed the source, what was concluded, which controls are affected and when the assessment must be revisited.

  • Named reviewer and review timestamp
  • Evidence snapshot and source version
  • Legal review status and limitations
  • Next review date and change trigger

Regulatory source categories

Examples of source classes, not legal conclusions
Government & licensing

Entity and operating permissions

Official legislation, government notifications, licensing portals, transport authority rules, local permits and published regulator guidance.

Road & vehicle

Road transport and safety

Vehicle registration, fitness, permits, driver qualification, road safety, dangerous-goods and local operating restrictions from competent authorities.

Rail, air & ocean

Mode-specific requirements

Relevant transport regulators, port and airport authorities, maritime administrations, rail authorities and applicable international instruments.

Customs & trade

Border and trade controls

Customs authorities, tariff and classification references, import/export controls, sanctions publications and official border procedures.

Warehousing & cargo

Facility and goods obligations

Facility permits, fire and building authorities, storage rules, bonded-warehouse requirements and commodity-specific controls.

Data & consumer

Privacy and commercial conduct

Official privacy and data-protection authorities, consumer protection, electronic records, competition, tax and employment sources.

Change detection and response

Regulatory change is an operational risk signal
Monitor

Scheduled source review

Assign risk-based review intervals and monitor official notices, amendment registers and regulator announcements. Do not assume automated monitoring is complete.

Assess

Impact analysis

Identify affected countries, lanes, services, partner credentials, customer terms and active market approvals. Escalate uncertain interpretation to qualified counsel.

Control

Update and notify

Version the source, reassess linked controls, suspend affected scope when necessary and notify accountable internal owners with an auditable outcome.

i
Primary sources first; legal interpretation remains accountable

Prefer official legislation, gazettes, regulator publications and competent authority portals. Secondary sources can help discovery but should not independently establish a legal requirement. Record uncertainty and obtain qualified local advice before consequential partner approval or market activation.
