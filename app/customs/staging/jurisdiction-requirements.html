Requirement governance workflow

Prevent stale or incomplete legal content from reaching operational decisions.

Source-to-publication lifecycle

Keep the legal source and review history attached to each requirement.

1
Identify scope and applicability

Define country, customs territory, zone, goods, procedure, parties and transaction date before researching a requirement.

2
Capture authoritative source

Record issuing body, official source reference, document title, version, publication and effective dates, retrieval time and relevant section.

3
Independent specialist review

Check interpretation, conflicts, exceptions, transition periods and applicability with an appropriately qualified reviewer.

4
Publish scoped requirement

State who and what it applies to, required evidence, deadlines, responsible role and limitations. Keep draft content visibly unpublished.

5
Monitor changes & archive

Recheck official sources, record amendments and supersession, notify affected workflows and retain historical versions.

E

Do not rely on the entry until it is revalidated or replaced.

Legal currency and operational relianceCustoms and trade rules can change by jurisdiction, product, origin, destination, procedure and transaction date. A portal link, cached document, AI summary or prior shipment outcome is not proof that a requirement remains current. Every published requirement needs an official source, effective date, retrieval/verification date, scope, reviewer and change history. Conflicts, missing sources and overdue reviews must be visibly flagged and excluded from automated decisions. Single-window access, credentials, declarations and authority responses require separate approved integrations and permissions; this page does not provide them.
