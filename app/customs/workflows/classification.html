Classification research workflow

Record the reasoning and source material behind every proposed code.

Research to controlled decision

A code suggestion is not a binding customs ruling.

1
Define the goods precisely

Capture composition, material, function, technical specifications, processing, intended use, condition and supporting product literature.

2
Identify the jurisdiction & date

Confirm the importing or exporting customs territory, applicable nomenclature edition and the date relevant to the transaction.

3
Research official classification sources

Consult the current official tariff, legal notes, explanatory material and relevant binding decisions where available. Preserve source, version and retrieval date.

4
Document reasoning & uncertainty

Record competing headings, applicable interpretative rules, evidence gaps and why the proposed classification fits the stated goods.

5
Independent qualified review

Capture reviewer identity, competence, decision scope, rationale, effective date and any need for an authority ruling.

Classification record essentials

Make each conclusion reproducible and limited to the goods reviewed.

A
Goods identity

Product description, composition, function, technical evidence and product/version identifiers.

B
Code structure

Record the HS level and any jurisdiction-specific national tariff extension separately.

C
Legal source trail

Source authority, legal instrument, nomenclature edition, effective date and archived reference.

D
Decision governance

Reviewer, rationale, confidence limitations, unresolved questions, approval scope and change history.

Classification and duty safeguardsHS classification can depend on detailed product facts, legal notes, interpretative rules, jurisdiction and effective date. An international HS heading does not by itself establish a complete national tariff code or duty rate. Do not infer duties, taxes, restrictions, eligibility or clearance outcomes from a proposed code. Where facts or sources are incomplete, retain “unresolved / not assessed” and refer the matter to a qualified customs professional or the competent authority.
