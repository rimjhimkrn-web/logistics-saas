Review workflow

Keep automated signals, evidence assessment and legal decisions distinct.

From transaction facts to disposition

Every stage requires traceability and accountable review.

1
Define the transaction scope

Capture relevant exporter, buyer, consignee, intermediaries, beneficial ownership where required, destination, transit, goods, end user and end use.

2
Select applicable control sources

Identify competent jurisdictions and authoritative lists, regulations, control schedules and licensing guidance. Record source owner, version, retrieval time and effective date.

3
Assess potential matches

Compare reliable identifiers and context. Similar names or incomplete identifiers are signals for review—not proof of a match or a basis for automatic rejection.

4
Review goods, end use and authorization

Assess classification, technical characteristics, destination, end-user and end-use restrictions, licence scope, conditions and validity with qualified specialists.

5
Record decision and reassessment

Document rationale, evidence, reviewer, approver, decision scope, restrictions, expiry and triggers for rescreening. Preserve the full audit history.

Decision states

Use explicit, non-ambiguous workflow states.

A
Not assessed

No valid assessment has been completed. This is not a clear result.

B
Evidence required

Identity, ownership, product, destination or end-use facts are incomplete or unreliable.

C
Potential match — review

A signal requires documented human investigation. Do not treat it as a confirmed match or false positive.

D
Escalated / legal review

Potential prohibition, restriction or unresolved ambiguity requires competent legal or compliance review.

E
Decision recorded

A named authorized reviewer records a scoped determination, evidence and conditions. It is not a universal permission to transact.

Fail-closed policy — absence of a match is not clearance. Missing data, unavailable lists, stale sources, failed integrations, ambiguous identities, empty search results or incomplete ownership information must never be interpreted as “screened clear”. Potential matches require human investigation; decisions must be scoped to the parties, goods, destination, end use, jurisdiction and time reviewed. Screening does not itself establish legal permission, issue a licence or authorize customs release. Do not auto-release, auto-book or auto-submit based on a screening result. Maintain source provenance, effective dates, reviewer identity, rationale, approvals and reassessment triggers.
