Shipment execution & service information

Clear handoffs. Visible movement.

This policy explains the operational information customers should receive for shipment preparation, pickup, transit, delivery and exceptions. The actual service scope, route, milestones, estimated dates and responsible provider are those expressly confirmed in the shipment-specific booking.

The booking confirmation must identify the contracting service provider, the agreed origin and destination, the selected transport service, the charges, the expected milestones and any special handling conditions.

1. Scope and service confirmation

This policy describes the general shipment information and handling principles for logistics services arranged through KRIM. It does not mean every transport mode, route, city, country or service is currently available. Availability depends on a verified provider, operating capacity, legal permissions, cargo acceptance and an explicit booking confirmation.

  • A quote request or indicative price is not a booking confirmation.
  • Only the confirmed booking defines the accepted service, route, schedule, provider and charges.
  • Any service-level commitment, delivery window or special handling promise must be expressly recorded in the booking documents.
  • Mandatory law and the accepted shipment-specific contract prevail where this general policy conflicts with them.

2. Booking, route and service details

Before accepting a shipment, review the origin and destination addresses, cargo description, quantity, dimensions, weight, packaging, pickup and delivery contacts, requested dates, service type and any access restrictions. The provider may require a site survey, loading details, vehicle suitability confirmation or additional documents before acceptance.

KRIM will display or communicate the details available in its workflow. Customers should report errors before dispatch. A route or mode shown as an option is not evidence of confirmed capacity until the booking is accepted by the authorised provider.

3. Cargo readiness and packaging

The shipper is responsible for accurately describing, packing, marking and preparing cargo in a manner suitable for the agreed transport and handling conditions, unless the booking expressly assigns a particular task to the provider. Cargo must be ready at the agreed location and within the agreed pickup window.

  • Use packaging appropriate to the cargo's weight, fragility, dimensions and handling risks.
  • Secure, label and segregate cargo where required; disclose fragile, hazardous, temperature-sensitive, high-value or otherwise special characteristics.
  • Provide accurate weight, dimensions, quantity, declared value and handling instructions.
  • Ensure the pickup location has safe access, loading facilities and authorised personnel where required.
  • Do not tender prohibited, illegal, undeclared or misdescribed goods.

Additional packaging, loading, labour, equipment or waiting services must be agreed and priced before they are performed, except where an emergency or law requires otherwise.

4. Pickup and cargo handover

Pickup dates and time windows are based on the accepted booking and provider availability. The shipper or authorised representative should be present, make cargo accessible and verify the count and condition recorded at handover. The provider should record the pickup or receipt event using the applicable consignment note, goods receipt, electronic scan or other shipment record.

Do not hand over cargo to an unverified person or vehicle. Where the vehicle, driver, quantity, seal, packaging condition or pickup details differ materially from the booking, pause handover where safe and contact the designated support channel. A pickup scan or receipt confirms the event recorded; it does not by itself establish final delivery or waive a claim for concealed damage.

5. Transit, handling and custody

During transit, cargo may pass through intermediate terminals, hubs, warehouses, transshipment points or subcontracted service providers where permitted by the applicable contract and law. The shipment documents should identify the contracting provider and relevant responsibility allocation. The actual custody chain may include more than one operator.

Transit estimates can be affected by distance, route conditions, traffic, weather, vehicle availability, terminal schedules, inspections, congestion, labour disruption, security events, government restrictions and other circumstances outside a provider's reasonable control. A revised estimate should be communicated when reliable information becomes available.

6. Tracking and shipment visibility

Where tracking events are available, KRIM may display status information received from the provider, operational systems or authorised integrations. Events may include booking accepted, pickup scheduled, picked up, in transit, arrived at facility, out for delivery, delivery attempted and delivered. The exact event set depends on the service and provider.

Tracking updates may be delayed, incomplete or corrected after reconciliation. A status display is an operational aid and not a guarantee of a vehicle's exact location, continuous GPS coverage, delivery time or cargo condition. For urgent discrepancies, contact KRIM with the shipment reference rather than relying only on a screen refresh.

7. Delivery and proof of delivery

Delivery is made to the consignee or an authorised recipient at the confirmed destination, subject to site access, identity/authority checks and the accepted service terms. The recipient should inspect the visible condition and count of packages before acknowledging receipt, and record any shortage, visible damage, broken seal or exception on the delivery document or electronic proof of delivery.

Proof of delivery may include a signed delivery note, recipient name, timestamp, photograph, OTP confirmation, electronic scan or other record used by the provider. The method depends on the service and applicable law. A proof-of-delivery record is evidence of the recorded delivery event; it does not automatically extinguish rights relating to concealed damage, fraud or other claims permitted by law.

8. Failed or unsuccessful delivery

Delivery may fail where the consignee is unavailable, the address is incorrect or inaccessible, the recipient refuses the cargo, required documents or duties are unpaid, the premises are closed, or a safety or legal restriction prevents handover. The provider should record the reason and follow the re-attempt, storage, return or escalation instructions in the booking.

Re-delivery, waiting, storage, return-to-origin or redirection charges may apply only where their basis was disclosed in the accepted booking or otherwise lawfully agreed, and where supported by provider records. The customer should respond promptly to failed-delivery notices to reduce avoidable costs.

9. Delays, service interruptions and exceptions

If a shipment is delayed, diverted, held, damaged by an incident or otherwise disrupted, the responsible provider should investigate and provide an update when reasonably practicable. KRIM will route information received through the platform and coordinate next steps within its actual contractual and operational role.

  • Weather, natural events, accidents, road closures and traffic disruption.
  • Vehicle, equipment, terminal, port, airport or infrastructure disruption.
  • Customs, regulatory, security or law-enforcement inspection or hold.
  • Incorrect or incomplete shipment information, documentation or consignee details.
  • Labour disruption, public emergency, government restriction or other force-majeure event.

These examples do not automatically excuse every failure. Responsibility and remedies depend on the facts, the contract and applicable mandatory law.

10. Changes, re-routing and address corrections

Requests to change the pickup or delivery address, consignee, delivery date, cargo details, route or service must be submitted promptly and are subject to provider approval, feasibility, security checks and any revised charges. Do not assume a change has been accepted until written confirmation is issued by the authorised party.

Some changes may be impossible after dispatch, customs filing, loading or arrival at a restricted facility. A change may require new documents, permits, declarations or customer authorisation.

11. Freight, waiting, storage and additional charges

The accepted quote and booking confirmation should set out the freight amount, applicable taxes and any included or excluded services. Potential additional charges may include loading or unloading, waiting time, detention, demurrage, tolls, parking, handling, storage, re-delivery, return transport, address correction, special equipment or regulatory charges, depending on the route and service.

Additional charges must be supported by the accepted terms, lawful authority or a later customer-approved change, and by reasonable supporting records. KRIM should not present an unconfirmed estimate as a final amount. The invoice or receipt must identify the supplier and payment collector for each component.

12. Restricted, regulated and special cargo

Customers must disclose cargo that is dangerous, hazardous, perishable, temperature-controlled, fragile, high-value, oversized, regulated, restricted or subject to special permits. Acceptance depends on provider capability, packaging, declarations, licences, route restrictions and applicable law. A provider may refuse cargo that is unsafe, prohibited, incorrectly declared or outside its authorised capability.

KRIM does not represent that any particular regulated or special cargo is accepted unless the relevant service provider has expressly confirmed it in writing. The customer remains responsible for accurate declarations and lawful tender of goods.

13. International and cross-border movements

International movements are available only where the route, service provider, legal permissions, customs process, insurance arrangements and payment method have been validated and expressly confirmed for the shipment. A country or currency appearing on the website does not establish active booking availability.

Before accepting an international shipment, the booking documents must identify the exporter/importer responsibilities, Incoterms where used, customs broker, required declarations, duties and taxes, inspection and storage exposure, restricted-goods rules, currency and any return or abandonment arrangements. Customs authorities may inspect, detain, reject or seize goods under applicable law.

14. Loss, damage, shortage and service complaints

Report visible damage, shortage, tampering or delivery exceptions to the driver/provider and record them on the delivery receipt before signing where possible. For concealed damage or loss discovered later, notify the responsible provider and KRIM promptly, preserve packaging and cargo evidence, and provide photographs, invoices, proof of value, shipment documents and other relevant records.

Liability, claim notice periods, exclusions, compensation limits and insurance cover depend on the actual carrier/provider contract, insurance policy and mandatory transport law. This policy does not create an insurance promise or override statutory rights. The responsible party and claim channel should be stated in the booking documents.

15. Support, policy limits and contact

For shipment status, pickup or delivery issues, use the KRIM Contact page and provide the shipment reference, route, event date and a concise description. Do not share passwords, OTPs, payment PINs or full card/bank credentials.

This general policy does not replace the accepted quote, consignment note, carrier terms, warehouse receipt, insurance wording or shipment-specific instructions. If documents conflict, the parties must resolve the discrepancy using the applicable contract and mandatory law. KRIM may update this policy as its verified network, operating procedures and legal requirements evolve; changes to an existing booking are governed by its applicable contract and law.

Operational readiness gate: Before presenting any route or delivery promise as bookable, KRIM must verify the provider's identity and capability, service permissions, route coverage, insurance where required, pickup/delivery process, tracking event source, proof-of-delivery method, exception escalation, charges, customer communications and applicable legal requirements. The publication of this policy does not prove that a route, provider, live tracking feed or delivery SLA is active.
