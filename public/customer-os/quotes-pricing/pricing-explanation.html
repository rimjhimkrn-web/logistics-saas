Pricing Explanation

Understand what makes up a logistics quote, which services each charge covers, what may change the final amount and what to confirm before accepting an offer.

← Return to quotesReview quote details
Read the actual quote first. The components below are a general guide, not KRIM tariffs or a promise that every charge applies. The issuing logistics partner’s quote, service scope and customer–provider agreement control the actual price.

Understand the components

A quote should explain the service basis and distinguish fixed, conditional and third-party charges.

01

Base transport

The primary movement charge. It may be priced per shipment, vehicle, container, kilogram, chargeable weight, distance or another stated unit.

Core service
02

Origin & destination handling

Pickup, loading, unloading, terminal handling, delivery or local movement. Check which locations and handling steps are included.

Scope-dependent
03

Fuel & market surcharges

Fuel, peak season, security, congestion or other variable surcharges. Confirm the calculation basis, effective period and whether they can change.

May vary
04

Customs & government charges

Brokerage, duties, taxes, inspection or statutory fees where applicable. Confirm the responsible party and whether amounts are estimates or assessed charges.

Jurisdiction-specific
05

Special services

Insurance, temperature control, hazardous-goods handling, oversized cargo, storage or other optional/special requirements when agreed.

Conditional / optional
06

Taxes & adjustments

Applicable taxes, discounts, credits, minimum charges and rounding. The quote should identify its tax treatment and the rules used to calculate totals.

What a clear quote should tell you

  • Who issued the quote and which legal entity provides the service.
  • Origin, destination, transport mode and service scope.
  • Charge units, quantities, minimums and measurement rules.
  • Included services, exclusions and conditional charges.
  • Currency, tax treatment, payment terms and quote validity.
  • Amendment, cancellation and additional-charge conditions.
  • How to ask questions or request a corrected quote.
Currency reminder: A display conversion is not a change to the quote’s transaction currency. Confirm the currency in which the provider expects payment and the applicable contractual FX rule, if any.

Before comparing or accepting

Compare equivalent service scopes and keep the original quote version available for review.

01 · Match scope

Confirm route, mode, cargo details, service level and shipment requirements.

02 · Check inclusions

Separate included services from conditional, optional and excluded charges.

03 · Verify terms

Check quote validity, currency, taxes, payment terms and amendment rules.

04 · Keep evidence

Retain the quote version, supporting breakdown and any written clarifications.

KRIM’s role in the transaction

KRIM connects customers with logistics partners; the selected partner provides the actual logistics service and issues its freight quote/invoice under the customer–provider arrangement. Any separately agreed KRIM partner marketing, referral or coordination fee is distinct from the provider’s freight charges. A quote display does not confirm partner capacity, route availability, a booking or service completion.
