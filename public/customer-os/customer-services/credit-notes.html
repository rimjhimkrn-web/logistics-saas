Customer finance · Invoice adjustments

Credit notes,
clearly accounted for.

A credit note records an authorized reduction or correction to an invoice. It should identify the original invoice, explain the adjustment and preserve a traceable financial record.

One adjustment. One clear audit trail.Original invoice reference · Issuing entity · Currency · Reason · Supporting evidence

Understand the credit note lifecycle

Key information to review before relying on an invoice adjustment.

↗

Who issued it?

Confirm the legal supplier that issued the original invoice. A credit note should come from the same issuing entity, subject to applicable law.

№

What is being adjusted?

Check the original invoice number, issue date, affected line items, adjustment amount, currency and applicable tax treatment.

✓

Why was it issued?

Review the stated reason, authorization, supporting documents and any required acknowledgement or notification.

Review sequence

Keep the source document and each subsequent action connected.

01 · IDENTIFYMatch the invoiceVerify the supplier, customer entity and original invoice reference.
02 · VALIDATECheck the adjustmentReview amounts, currency, tax lines, reason and evidence.
03 · AUTHORIZEConfirm approvalUse the issuer's authorized process and retain the approval record.
04 · RECONCILEUpdate the balanceReconcile the credit against the invoice without erasing its history.

Important distinction: In KRIM's partner-direct operating model, the logistics provider contracts with the customer and issues its own freight invoice and any related credit note. Those provider documents are managed by that provider and may not appear in KRIM's invoice register. KRIM should issue a credit note only for an invoice legally issued by KRIM itself. Tax treatment, required fields, deadlines and retention rules vary by jurisdiction; obtain appropriate professional advice. Currency should be identified using its ISO 4217 code, and the original invoice and audit history should remain traceable.
