Six essential control domains

Use these as a practical review framework, not as a certification claim.

01

Identity & authentication

Ensure every team member has an individually attributable identity and a secure authentication method.

  • Use unique accounts; prohibit shared credentials.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication where available or required.
  • Protect recovery channels and end sessions on shared devices.
  • Remove access promptly when a person leaves the organization.
02

Organization isolation

Prevent one partner organization from accessing another organization's confidential data.

  • Scope every request to an authorized organization membership.
  • Enforce access in server-side authorization and database RLS.
  • Never trust a browser-supplied organization ID alone.
  • Test cross-organization access denial before release.
03

Roles & least privilege

Grant only the permissions needed for each person's actual operational responsibilities.

  • Separate finance, operations, driver and administrator duties.
  • Require authorized review for sensitive changes.
  • Prevent users from approving their own restricted actions.
  • Review elevated access periodically.
04

Secrets & integrations

Protect credentials, API integrations, webhooks and sensitive business information.

  • Keep service-role keys and private secrets off the client.
  • Store secrets in an approved server-side secret manager.
  • Rotate exposed or expired credentials and revoke unused keys.
  • Verify webhook signatures and reject replayed events.
05

Audit evidence & retention

Maintain reliable records for sensitive actions, reviews and operational changes.

  • Record actor, organization, action, timestamp and outcome.
  • Restrict audit-log modification and deletion.
  • Redact passwords, tokens and unnecessary personal data.
  • Define retention and lawful evidence-access procedures.
06

Incident response

Make security concerns reportable, reviewable and actionable through a controlled process.

  • Identify a responsible security contact and escalation path.
  • Preserve relevant timestamps and request references.
  • Contain affected credentials or integrations safely.
  • Document investigation, corrective action and closure approval.

Security incident handling sequence

A simple response structure for partner teams to adapt to their approved policies.

A

Report & preserve

Record when the issue was noticed, the affected workflow, a safe reference ID and the reporting contact. Preserve relevant evidence without circulating secrets.

B

Contain & assess

The authorized security owner assesses scope and impact, then safely restricts affected sessions, credentials or integrations where needed.

C

Recover & validate

Restore only after corrective action and appropriate checks. Confirm that normal access and business operations are safe to resume.

D

Document & close

Keep a reviewable record of decisions, approvals, communications and follow-up actions under the organization's retention rules.

Security questions

Important boundaries for partner administrators and team members.

Does this page verify that my partner account is secure?

No. This is a static staging preview. Actual verification requires authenticated security checks, server-side controls, database-policy tests and review of relevant evidence.

Can a partner administrator see every audit event?

Not automatically. Production audit access should be limited by role, organization scope, data classification and applicable retention rules.

Should API keys or passwords be attached to an audit report?

No. Never include passwords, OTPs, private keys, service-role keys or full access tokens in screenshots, exports, support tickets or audit evidence.

What must be tested before this page is connected to production?

Verify authentication, organization-level RLS, role permissions, cross-tenant denial, audit immutability, safe field selection, export authorization, error handling and security event logging.

Need help with a security concern?

Use KRIM's official contact channel. Share a safe reference only—never send credentials or secrets.
