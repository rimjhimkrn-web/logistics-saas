Platform terms & service conditions

Clear terms. Responsible movement.

These terms explain access to KRIM’s digital platform, logistics enquiries, quotations, booking confirmations and service-provider relationships. Each accepted movement must also have transaction-specific terms identifying the responsible contracting parties, services, charges and remedies.

Please read these terms before using a KRIM account or submitting a service request. Additional terms may apply to a particular service, route, partner, payment method or booking. If a specific written booking confirmation conflicts with these general terms, the booking confirmation governs that shipment only to the extent of the stated conflict and where legally valid.

1. Agreement and scope

These terms apply to access and use of the KRIM Logistics website and digital platform, including customer, partner and business workflows. By browsing public information, you are not automatically entering a transport contract. If you create an account, submit a request or accept a quote, you agree to the provisions relevant to that action, subject to any separate terms presented for the transaction. Mandatory rights under consumer, transport, contract, data-protection and other applicable laws remain unaffected.

If you use the platform for an organisation, you confirm that you are authorised to act for it. If you do not agree to the applicable terms, do not use the relevant account or booking workflow.

2. KRIM’s role and the logistics service provider

KRIM provides a digital logistics platform and related commercial workflows through which customers may describe shipment requirements, request quotations and connect with transport, warehouse and other service providers. The precise legal role KRIM performs in a particular movement depends on the actual service design, documents, representations, control exercised and applicable law—not merely the label used on this website.

Before a customer commits, the booking confirmation must identify the contracting party or parties, the party responsible for physical carriage or other fulfilment, KRIM’s own agreed role and charges, the service scope, applicable provider terms and the route for support and claims. Where a provider independently performs the physical service, that provider must be identified. Where KRIM contracts as principal, carrier, booking company, agent, broker or another regulated role, the relevant obligations and registrations must be satisfied and disclosed as required by law.

Important legal classification: Indian transport and e-commerce laws can apply based on the actual business activity. For example, the Carriage by Road Act, 2007 includes certain goods-booking companies, contractors, agents and brokers within its definition of “common carrier”. KRIM must obtain transaction-specific legal advice and verify any registration or authorisation required before offering or arranging regulated carriage. Describing a provider as independent does not, by itself, remove KRIM’s statutory responsibilities.
Availability: A route, country, mode or provider is available only when the relevant operational capability has been checked and KRIM confirms availability for the specific request. A quote request, indicative estimate or partner bid is not a confirmed booking.

3. Marketplace transparency and consumer protection

Where KRIM’s service or transaction is subject to consumer-protection or e-commerce requirements, KRIM will provide the disclosures and grievance facilities required by the law applicable to that transaction. Information material to a customer’s decision—including the contracting provider, service description, total payable price and applicable taxes, payment method, delivery estimate, cancellation/refund conditions, insurance position and complaint route—should be presented clearly before acceptance.

KRIM will not use misleading descriptions, fabricated availability, deceptive interface patterns or undisclosed sponsored placement. Any ranking, recommendation, promotion or commercial relationship that legally requires disclosure should be identified appropriately. Customer information will be used and shared in accordance with the Privacy Notice, applicable law and any required affirmative consent.

The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026 are scheduled to take effect on 1 January 2027. KRIM will review and implement applicable requirements before that date, including any relevant grievance, search/ranking, sponsored-listing, pricing, dark-pattern, seller/provider disclosure and consumer-information requirements. The precise obligations depend on KRIM’s legal classification and the service actually offered.

Business-model and legal-form gate: If KRIM is classified as an “e-commerce entity” under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, Rule 4(1)(a), as substituted in 2021, states that the entity shall be a company incorporated under the Companies Act (or a specified foreign company/office/branch/agency). KRIM is currently structured as a sole proprietorship. This creates a material eligibility question if KRIM operates the platform as an e-commerce entity. Before enabling marketplace-style paid bookings, KRIM must obtain written Indian legal advice on the correct classification and legal structure, and must not assume that a proprietorship satisfies this rule. The 2026 amendments do not remove the need to assess the underlying eligibility requirements.
Transport authorisation gate: The Carriage by Road Act, 2007 defines “common carrier” broadly and expressly includes certain goods booking companies, contractors, agents and brokers. If KRIM’s actual activity falls within that definition, the Act requires registration. A partner-led model or contract label does not automatically avoid this requirement. Obtain a written assessment and complete any required registration before offering or arranging regulated carriage.

4. Accounts, access and acceptable use

  • Provide accurate and current account, contact and organisation details.
  • Keep credentials and authentication codes confidential; promptly report suspected unauthorised access.
  • Use only the roles and permissions assigned to you. Do not access, scrape, copy or interfere with another user’s data or account.
  • Do not upload malicious code, attempt to bypass security, disrupt platform operations or misuse the platform for unlawful activity.
  • Keep business, driver, vehicle, licence and other onboarding information accurate and update material changes.

KRIM may apply verification, role restrictions and reasonable security checks. The platform is designed around nine user types: Customer/Shipper, Logistics Partner/Carrier, Driver, Warehouse Operator, Customs & Trade, Finance, Operations Control Tower, Enterprise and Platform Administrator. Each account receives only the workspace and permissions authorised for its identity and organisation. Platform Administrator access is private and restricted; it is not a public customer or partner sign-in route. A role label or visible navigation item does not itself grant access—authorisation must be enforced by authenticated, server-side permissions. Account access is not a guarantee that a user or partner has been independently certified for every service or route.

5. Customer and shipper responsibilities

Customers are responsible for providing complete and accurate shipment information, including pickup and delivery addresses, contact details, cargo description, quantity, dimensions, weight, packaging, handling requirements, declared value and any special instructions. You must identify fragile, temperature-controlled, hazardous, regulated, high-value or otherwise exceptional cargo before requesting a quote.

You must ensure that you own the goods or are authorised to arrange their movement, that goods are properly packed and labelled, and that required permits, declarations and documents are genuine and available. You must make the goods accessible for the agreed collection and delivery windows and ensure an authorised recipient is available at destination.

6. Quotations, acceptance and booking formation

A quotation is based on the shipment details, route, service level, capacity and market information available when it is prepared. It may be subject to inspection, measurement, weight verification, address validation, partner confirmation, taxes, tolls, fuel changes, waiting time or other disclosed surcharges. Any assumptions, exclusions and quote-validity period should be shown with the quote.

A request for a quote, an indicative estimate, an automated screen or a partner bid is not by itself a confirmed booking. A booking is formed only when the relevant contracting party or parties issue or record an explicit acceptance/confirmation after provider, service scope, total price, payment and operational checks have been completed. The customer should receive a booking reference and the identity and contact route of the contracting service provider, together with KRIM’s role in the transaction.

If actual cargo or service conditions differ materially from the information supplied, the quote may need to be revised. No revised charge should be treated as accepted without the customer’s agreement, except where a charge is already clearly authorised in the booking terms or required by law.

7. Independent partner responsibilities

Partners are responsible for maintaining all licences, registrations, permits, insurance and operational authorisations required for the services they offer. They must provide accurate capability, vehicle, equipment, driver, capacity, service-area and pricing information, and promptly disclose any restriction or change that affects an accepted task.

  • Perform accepted work with reasonable care, skill and in accordance with applicable law and the confirmed service scope.
  • Maintain appropriate vehicle/equipment roadworthiness, driver qualifications, safety procedures and cargo-handling standards.
  • Protect customer and shipment information and use it only for the assigned operational purpose or another lawful basis.
  • Provide truthful status updates, proof of pickup/delivery and prompt notice of incidents, delay, damage, loss or inability to perform.
  • Do not subcontract or reassign an accepted task where prohibited by the booking terms or without required approval.

Partner onboarding, profile visibility or a platform account is not a guarantee of a partner’s performance, solvency, insurance coverage or suitability for every shipment. Any verification performed by KRIM is limited to the checks expressly described and does not replace a customer’s own due diligence.

8. Cargo restrictions, documents and regulatory compliance

You must not request movement of unlawful, prohibited, undeclared or improperly packaged goods. Dangerous, hazardous, controlled, perishable, temperature-sensitive, oversized, high-value or restricted goods may be accepted only if the service is legally permitted, the provider is suitably authorised and the required declarations, packaging, handling and approvals are confirmed in writing.

Customers and partners must comply with applicable transport, road safety, labour, customs, import/export, sanctions, tax and other regulatory requirements relevant to their role and shipment. The party responsible for a declaration, permit, classification or customs filing must ensure its accuracy. KRIM does not provide legal, customs-brokerage or tax advice unless a separate written agreement expressly says otherwise.

KRIM or the provider may refuse, hold or stop a shipment where information is missing, cargo appears unsafe or unlawful, required documents are absent, or continuing would create a legal or safety risk. Any resulting storage, return, disposal or other costs will be handled according to the booking terms and applicable law.

9. Pricing, payments, taxes and records

Prices, currency, included services, taxes, surcharges, payment deadlines, refund conditions and the payment recipient must be disclosed in the applicable quote or booking confirmation. Do not rely on an estimate or a price shown for a different route or shipment.

Payments may be processed through a third-party payment provider using its terms and security processes. KRIM may receive transaction references and payment status for reconciliation. A payment is treated as received only when the relevant provider confirms it and KRIM’s transaction records are reconciled, where applicable. Never share UPI PINs, passwords, card security codes or one-time passwords with KRIM or a logistics partner.

Each party is responsible for taxes, invoices and statutory records allocated to it by law and the transaction documents. The invoice issuer, GST treatment, tax rate and recipient must be clear for each supply; KRIM must not imply that it issues a carrier’s invoice or that a partner issues KRIM’s platform-fee invoice unless the transaction documents and tax treatment support that result. Any platform fee, partner payout, deduction or split settlement must be clearly disclosed and is subject to payment-provider approval and applicable law. No split-settlement arrangement is promised unless enabled and confirmed for that transaction.

10. Booking changes, cancellation and refunds

Requests to change cargo details, route, addresses, timing, vehicle type or service scope are subject to provider capacity and written confirmation. A change may affect price, timing and feasibility. Do not assume a requested change has taken effect until it is confirmed.

Cancellation windows, charges, non-refundable costs and refund timing must be stated in the quote or booking confirmation before acceptance. They may depend on whether a provider has been assigned, a vehicle dispatched, pickup completed or third-party costs incurred. If the booking documents do not clearly disclose the applicable cancellation terms, KRIM should resolve that omission before accepting payment or the booking.

Approved refunds are returned through the original payment method where possible and remain subject to payment-provider processing timelines and any mandatory legal requirements. The detailed process and stage-based review are set out in the Refund & Cancellation Policy; the accepted booking confirmation must state the applicable transaction-specific terms.

11. Delivery, delays, loss, damage and claims

Any estimated pickup or delivery time is an estimate unless the booking expressly guarantees a time and states the remedy for missing it. Customers must inspect goods at delivery where reasonably possible, record visible exceptions on the delivery receipt and notify the relevant provider and KRIM promptly of loss, shortage, damage or delay.

Claims must be submitted through the process and within the time limit stated in the applicable provider contract, transport document, insurance policy or mandatory law. Provide shipment references, photographs, delivery records and reasonable supporting evidence. KRIM can help route a claim or share available records where agreed, but the responsible contracting provider or insurer must be identified in the booking documents. Any statutory notice period, liability regime or remedy applicable to a common carrier or other regulated provider prevails over inconsistent wording here.

Nothing in these terms removes a statutory right or mandatory claim period. Do not rely on this general page for a shipment-specific insurance limit or claims deadline.

12. Liability and insurance

Liability for physical carriage, storage, handling, loss, damage and delay depends on the identity of the contracting service provider, the agreed service, applicable transport documents, insurance terms and mandatory law. The provider’s applicable liability terms and claims route must be disclosed before booking.

KRIM’s liability, if any, for its own platform or separately agreed coordination services must be assessed under the specific contract and applicable law. Nothing in these terms excludes or limits liability where such exclusion or limitation is prohibited, including liability that cannot lawfully be excluded for fraud, wilful misconduct, death, personal injury or other non-excludable matters.

Insurance is not automatically included merely because a shipment is arranged through the platform. Coverage, insured party, exclusions, declared value, premium, claim procedure and insurer must be confirmed in writing for the shipment. Customers should arrange suitable insurance where needed.

13. Events outside reasonable control

A party is not treated as in breach to the extent performance is prevented by an event beyond its reasonable control, such as severe weather, flood, fire, earthquake, epidemic, war, civil unrest, government action, border closure, strike, major infrastructure failure or widespread network outage. The affected party should notify the other parties as soon as reasonably practicable, explain the expected impact and take reasonable steps to reduce disruption.

This section does not excuse payment obligations already due, failure to take reasonable precautions, or duties that cannot legally be suspended. If disruption continues, the parties should follow the cancellation, return, storage and refund provisions in the booking documents and applicable law.

14. Geographic availability and international movements

KRIM’s initial commercial focus is India. The platform may be architected for multiple countries, currencies, languages and transport modes, but a technical feature or country listing is not proof that a market, route, customs workflow or provider is operational.

International or cross-border movements are subject to route-specific partner validation, required licences, customs arrangements, sanctions screening, export/import documentation, tax treatment, insurance, payment availability and written acceptance. A shipment request may be declined if any required capability or compliance condition is not satisfied.

You receive a limited, revocable, non-exclusive right to use the platform for its intended lawful purpose. You may not copy, resell, reverse engineer, scrape or commercially exploit platform content or functionality except where applicable law permits or KRIM gives written permission.

You retain rights in information and materials you submit. You grant KRIM the limited permission needed to host, process, display and share those materials with relevant providers to operate the requested workflow and meet legal obligations. You confirm you have the authority to provide them.

16. Suspension, refusal and termination

KRIM may restrict, suspend or close an account, refuse a request or pause a workflow where reasonably necessary for security, suspected fraud, unlawful activity, safety, inaccurate information, non-payment, partner failure, regulatory concerns or breach of these terms. Where appropriate and lawful, KRIM will provide notice and an opportunity to address the issue.

Suspension does not remove accrued payment obligations, rights relating to an accepted shipment, statutory rights or provisions that by their nature should continue, including confidentiality, records, claims and dispute provisions.

Raise an operational complaint first through the KRIM Contact page, identifying the account or shipment reference and the issue. KRIM will review matters within the scope of its role and, where appropriate, route the issue to the contracting service provider.

These terms and the platform relationship are intended to be governed by the laws of India, subject to mandatory consumer, transport, data-protection and other applicable laws. No exclusive court location or mandatory arbitration clause is imposed by this page. The competent courts and any agreed dispute-resolution process must be confirmed in the final legal version after the legal operator’s principal place of business and transaction structure are verified. Nothing here removes a consumer’s right to approach a forum that has jurisdiction under mandatory law.

For material changes affecting an existing booking, the applicable booking documents and mandatory law will determine whether fresh notice or agreement is required.
