How the workflow connects

From request to completed movement.

A controlled transaction path helps prevent an enquiry from being mistaken for a confirmed shipment.

Shipment enquiry

Customer submits origin, destination, cargo and service requirements.

Rate & partner check

Check applicable commercial terms or invite an eligible partner to quote.

Review & acceptance

Customer reviews a valid offer; booking proceeds only after required checks.

Payment & execution

Complete applicable payment steps and coordinate the confirmed service.

Milestones & delivery

Record available progress, exceptions and delivery confirmation.

Connected workspaces

Every participant has a defined role.

KRIM's workspace model separates customer activity, partner execution and internal coordination. Access should be granted according to verified identity, organisation membership and role permissions.

  • Customer and enterprise shipment management
  • Partner opportunities, quotations and assignments
  • Driver and warehouse operational updates
  • Customs, finance and control-tower coordination
  • Private administration — never exposed as a public workspace

Honest operating status

Global-ready design. Real-world activation, market by market.

KRIM's architecture is intended to support multiple countries, currencies, languages and regulatory workflows. That does not mean every lane, provider or local process is already live.

Platform foundations

Digital workflow foundations

Public shipment enquiry, authenticated customer and partner workspaces, shipment and quote workflow components, and a public tracking interface are present in the codebase. End-to-end production operation still requires live testing.

Activation required

Service and market availability

Rates, provider capacity, accepted assignments, live milestone feeds, customs coverage, settlement and cross-border service must be verified for each route before being offered as available.
